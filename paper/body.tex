\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Interactive artificial intelligence (AI) tables turn a natural-language
prompt into a \emph{materialized view}: a column in which a language
model computes every cell from the prompt and that cell's row. Each
cell is one draw from the model's output distribution
(\secref{sec:problem} gives the notation). The
category is now a product surface rather than a research proposal.
Google Sheets evaluates an \texttt{AI()} formula per row and offers AI
columns in tables~\cite{sheetsai}, Airtable computes AI fields per
record~\cite{airtableai}, Notion autofills database properties with a
model~\cite{notionautofill}, and Snowflake Cortex applies
\texttt{AI\_COMPLETE} and \texttt{AI\_CLASSIFY} to table columns in
SQL~\cite{snowflakecortex}. Microsoft's Excel \texttt{COPILOT} function
shipped as a preview and was withdrawn in September 2026, a measure of
how quickly this surface is moving~\cite{excelcopilot}. These views are
edited constantly: users iterate on prompts the way they iterate on
spreadsheet formulas. Every edit invalidates the cache by construction,
and today's systems invalidate it wholesale: an edit to an
\corpusSO-row column recomputes all \corpusSO\ cells, or the user
silently keeps stale values with no bound on how wrong they are.

Recent semantic data systems bound the error of \emph{one-shot} query
execution against a reference model whose answers are taken as correct
(cascades, semantic operators, approximate processing with guarantees).
Verified semantic caches bound reuse under a \emph{fixed} definition
(\secref{sec:related} names both lines). Neither answers the maintenance
question an interactive table poses: \emph{after this specific
edit $\pi \to \pi'$, which cached cells are still valid, and with what
guarantee?}

\subsection{Running example}
One column from our evaluation recurs
throughout. Over \corpusSO\ Stack Overflow (SO) developer profiles, a
Boolean AI column asks \emph{``Is this person a data-platform
specialist?''}, judged from each profile's role, databases, platforms,
and tools. Its owner edits the prompt to insert two paragraph breaks
and lowercase one word, changing no content word (the
\emph{formatting-only} edit of \secref{sec:experiments};
\secref{sec:benchmark} lists every edit we study). Recomputing means
computing all \corpusSO\ cells again. Keeping every cached value
silently carries the edit's flip rate into the table: the share of cells
a recompute would change, \flipSoFormatting\ on this pair.
\sivm{} instead groups the cached cells by their cached value (the two
\emph{strata}, cached-TRUE and cached-FALSE), recomputes a small seeded
sample from each, and reuses a stratum only when a statistical bound on
its flip rate clears the user's error budget $\alpha$. The procedure
errs with probability at most $\delta$. On this column, at $\alpha{=}0.2$ and
$\delta{=}0.1$, \deployOracle\ fresh calls certify reuse of
\deployReused\ of the \deployN\ cells. A live run did so
\driftDaysLater\ days after the column was built, against a model that
had drifted in the meantime, and the draws stored when the column was
built lead to the same decision (\secref{sec:exp-deploy}). The cells it
refuses are the cached-TRUE stratum, which flips at \minoritySingle\
under the same edit on the labeled evaluation rows. A single bound over
all the cells would clear the budget and reuse those cells too
(\secref{sec:exp-baselines}).

\subsection{Why intuition fails here}
Three measurements reframe the problem (\secref{sec:experiments}).
First, large language model (LLM) cells are stochastic even at
temperature zero~\cite{ouyang,atil}: the same
prompt on the same row disagrees with itself \floorBool\ of the time on
our Boolean workload and \floorDefaultT\ at default temperature
(\secref{sec:experiments} gives the measurement behind each figure).
``Did the edit change
this cell?'' is therefore only meaningful relative to a noise floor, the
rate at which the model disagrees with itself under the unedited
prompt. Second, edit
direction is not monotone: under a strictly scope-\emph{widening} edit,
\wideningTrueFlip\ of
cached-TRUE cells flipped, refuting the folk lemma that widening
preserves positives. Third, an edit's surface class is not a safe
predictor of its semantic impact. On the same column, a synonym
paraphrase written to preserve meaning flipped \flipSoSynonym\ of cells
where a formatting-only edit flipped \flipSoFormatting. On a candidate-profile column from a second corpus, a
formatting-only edit flipped \flipDjFormatting\ against
\flipDjCriteria\ for a deliberate criteria change, rates that are
indistinguishable. Neither pair orders the way the edits' labels
suggest. The study's five edits are author-written, one per class per
column, so they show that class can fail to predict flip rate, not how
often it does. The certifier below never consults the class, and
\secref{sec:experiments} shows the same edit's flip rate varying widely
across models. Both comparisons hold the column fixed
deliberately: on the two column types we study, the column's own
stability moves the flip rate more than the edit does.

Sound reuse therefore has to be \emph{measured}: sample a few fresh
cells, statistically bound the flip rate of the rest, and reuse only
what clears the user's error budget.

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Problem formalization} (\secref{sec:problem}): certified
cross-version reuse for stochastic prompt-defined views. Two estimands
(the population quantities a guarantee is about) are well-defined under
decode pinning. One is the realized false-reuse rate over the cells a
certified stratum presents, which we certify by default. The other is
the same rate over the reused cells alone, which the strict mode
certifies at a correspondingly lower threshold. An impossibility floor
follows: with the prompt unedited, a budget $\alpha$ below a stratum's
self-flip rate is rarely certifiable, and \secref{sec:problem} bounds
how rarely.
\item \textbf{The \sivm{} procedure} (\emph{semantic incremental view
maintenance}; \secref{sec:system}): a frozen stratification, an
adaptive sample, and a futility rule. The stratification is by cached
value, optionally refined by how strongly the edit's changed terms
match each row. Sampling follows a
doubling schedule of \emph{looks} (points at which the sample is
inspected). The futility rule gives up on a stratum whose sample
already exceeds the budget. Certification is per-stratum with an
exact finite-population confidence bound, and the procedure offers two
guarantee targets (presented cells; strict reuse set). Deciding at several looks is
\emph{peeking}; the validity theorem survives it by splitting the
failure probability $\delta$ across the looks. Any frozen stratifier affects power
only, never validity, where power is the chance of certifying a stratum
that does meet the budget. The bound is a pluggable component: the exact
bound is proven for the without-replacement sampling the procedure
performs, and four alternatives (two empirical-Bernstein bounds, the
binomial Clopper--Pearson bound, and a betting confidence sequence) are
measured inside the same procedure.
\item \textbf{A working implementation} (\secref{sec:impl}) inside an
AI-table system, released under a source-available noncommercial
license. The system has a per-cell runner, a content-hash cache, a cost
ledger, and certificates (the persisted records of what cleared the
budget), which the user interface (UI) surfaces.
\item \textbf{An empirical anatomy} (\secref{sec:experiments}) of one
primary model on two real corpora with \labeledCells\ labeled cells. It
measures the noise floor, the savings frontier over the error budget,
and minority-stratum volatility, along with how much of it majority
voting at write time removes. It includes a stratifier ablation
(refinement leaves validity intact but costs power on this benchmark)
and a calibration study of how often a certificate exceeds its budget,
against the nominal $\delta$. \ifnum\indepAvailable=1\relax It checks
the cross-row independence assumption. \fi It also covers \famCount\
further models from four providers. A re-certification
\driftDaysLater\ days after the column was built measured drift
of the served model as a higher flip rate against the same cache, and
certified both budgets again. \ifnum\pilotAvailable=1\relax A free-text
pilot (\secref{sec:limits}) calibrates a judge's error on hand labels
and folds it into the budget.\fi
\item \textbf{A reusable benchmark} (\secref{sec:benchmark}): five
versioned prompt-edit pairs with per-cell flip labels over public
corpora redistributable under the Open Database License (ODbL) and the
MIT License, enabling like-for-like evaluation of future maintenance
systems.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Terminology.} Table~\ref{tab:notation} defines the terms
and the symbols this article uses. Four terms carry most of the weight.
A \emph{stratum} is a group of cached cells fixed before any sampling
(by default, the cells that share a cached value). A \emph{look} is a
point in the sampling schedule at which the sample drawn so far is
inspected and a decision may be taken. A stratum's \emph{floor} is its
self-flip rate, the rate at which the model disagrees with its own
earlier answer when nothing has changed. A \emph{certificate} is the
persisted record that a stratum cleared the budget, with the evidence it
cleared it on.

\paragraph{Views and cells.} An AI column is a view $V=(\pi, \mathcal{M}, \theta)$:
a prompt template $\pi$ with a typed output contract, a model $\mathcal{M}$, and
pinned decode parameters $\theta$. For row $r_i$, the bound prompt
$\pi(r_i)$ interpolates only the fields $\beta(\pi)$ that $\pi$
references; the stored cell $c_i$ is \emph{one draw} from the output
distribution $f_V(r_i)$. Even at temperature zero this distribution is
not degenerate: providers are nondeterministic across calls, as prior
measurements of hosted models document~\cite{ouyang,atil}, in part
because the floating-point reduction order inside serving kernels
changes with the co-batched load~\cite{yuan,tml}. Every quantity
below is therefore defined over draw randomness.

\paragraph{The maintenance event.} The user edits the definition,
$V \to V' = (\pi', \mathcal{M}, \theta)$. For each cached cell define the flip
indicator
$F_i \;=\; \mathbf{1}\!\left[\, c_i \not\equiv X_i' \,\right]$,
where $X_i' \sim f_{V'}(r_i)$ is a fresh draw under the new definition
and $\equiv$ is the column's equivalence relation (exact match for the
Boolean and select columns studied here). The indicator $F_i$ is
Bernoulli with unknown mean $p_i = \Pr[c_i \not\equiv X_i']$. That mean
deliberately folds together the edit's semantic effect and decode noise
on both sides: it answers the operational question of whether
recomputing cell $i$ now would return a value different from the one
shown.

\paragraph{Estimand and guarantee.} A maintenance policy partitions the
cached cells into a sampled set $\mathcal{S}$ (freshly recomputed), a
reuse set $\mathcal{R}$ (stale values kept), and a recompute set. Two
error rates matter, and they differ. The \emph{presented-cells} rate of
a certified stratum $j$ is
\[
  \mathrm{FR}^{\mathrm{pres}}_j \;=\; \tfrac{1}{n_j}
  \textstyle\sum_{i \in j \setminus \mathcal{S}} F_i :
\]
of all $n_j$ cells the user now sees from that stratum, the fraction
that would flip if recomputed (sampled cells are fresh and contribute
zero). That zero is a convention of the single-draw estimand, not a
property of recomputed cells: a recomputed cell is itself one draw, and
a further draw would disagree with it at about the stratum's self-flip
rate. A flip is any disagreement with one fresh draw, decode noise
included, as the previous paragraph defines it. The estimand charges
reuse for exactly what the user would see change on recomputation,
which is the operational question. The impossibility floor below is the
price of that choice: an excess-over-floor target, $p_j - s_j$, would
certify the identity edit for free. We deliberately do not adopt that
target, because a user who recomputes sees one draw and lives with it.
The \emph{reuse-set} rate
\[
  \mathrm{FR}^{\mathrm{reuse}}_j \;=\; \tfrac{1}{n_j - m_j}
  \textstyle\sum_{i \in j\setminus\mathcal{S}} F_i
\]
normalizes by the $n_j{-}m_j$ reused cells alone and is never
smaller. A certificate bounds the stratum's whole flip count, and hence
$\mathrm{FR}^{\mathrm{pres}}_j$, but not $\mathrm{FR}^{\mathrm{reuse}}_j$:
the remainder is adversely selected, because certification happens
exactly when the sample looked clean. \sivm{} therefore
certifies the presented-cells estimand by default, and offers a strict
reuse-set mode that certifies at the lower (\emph{deflated}) threshold
$\alpha (n_j - m_j)/n_j$, which bounds $\mathrm{FR}^{\mathrm{reuse}}_j$
by conservation of flips. On the \emph{headline pair}, the SO
formatting-only edit of the running example, at $\alpha{=}0.2$ the two
rates read \benchRealized\ and \benchRealizedReuse\
(Table~\ref{tab:main}). Theorem~\ref{thm:valid} states both
guarantees for the realized rates of this run, not for their
expectations. Table~\ref{tab:main} reports both realized rates for the
main results. All guarantees are per-stratum and therefore
hold simultaneously for every union of certified strata, not only the
aggregate (baseline B2 in \secref{sec:exp-baselines}, aggregate-only
certification, shows why the aggregate alone is operationally
misleading).

\sivm{} makes no decision about an individual cell. Whether one cached
cell would change under the edited prompt can be learned only by
recomputing it, which spends exactly the call that reuse would save.
The cheapest per-cell signal, prompt similarity, predicts flips at
chance across a definition change (baseline B1 in
\secref{sec:exp-baselines}). The signals that do carry information, the
cached value and how strongly the edit's changed terms match the row
(the interaction score of \secref{sec:system}), separate groups of
cells, not single cells, and enter as strata. The unit of decision is therefore
the stratum: an unsampled cached cell is reused if and only if the
upper confidence bound on its stratum's flip rate clears the budget at
a look the schedule reaches. That is the correctness criterion for
reuse, and it
is a statement about recomputation, not about ground truth. A
certificate asserts that recomputing the stratum now would change at
most an $\alpha$ share of its cells. It does not assert that the cells
are right.

\paragraph{Reading the budget.} The guarantee is a statement about the
procedure, over a run's sampling and oracle draws. With probability at
least $1-\delta$ the run issues no wrong certificate. In the default
mode a wrong certificate is one for a stratum in which more than an
$\alpha$ fraction of the cells would flip if recomputed. In the strict
mode it is one for a stratum in which more than an $\alpha$ fraction of
the reused cells would. The guarantee is not the probability that a
certificate in hand is right. A stratum whose flip
rate sits just above the budget is certified rarely, and every
certificate it does receive is wrong; Table~\ref{tab:main} contains
such a configuration (\secref{sec:exp-main}). Both $\alpha$ and
$\delta$ are the user's to set, and the
right values are workload-specific. A list that a person keeps working
through tolerates a looser $\alpha$ than a column that gates a decision
or feeds a downstream aggregate, and the strict mode exists for the
latter (\secref{sec:limits}). The
realized error under a certificate is typically far below $\alpha$
(\benchRealized\ against $\alpha{=}0.2$ on the headline pair,
Table~\ref{tab:main}), because the bound must cover the worst
population compatible with the sample. The frontier of
\secref{sec:exp-main} shows what tightening $\alpha$ costs in
certifiable savings, and the floor below shows where tightening it
becomes impossible.

\paragraph{Assumptions.}
\begin{assumption}[Pinned snapshot]\label{as:pin}
The model $\mathcal{M}$ and the decode parameters $\theta$ are fixed
during certification. Oracle draws are
distributed as $f_{V'}(r_i)$ throughout the certification window and
are independent \emph{across rows}. Provider-side batching that
correlates concurrent responses~\cite{yuan,tml} would violate this; our
runner issues independent requests\ifnum\indepAvailable=1\relax, and
\secref{sec:exp-validity} compares sequential with concurrent
requests\fi. The validity proof uses independence only through
non-interference: a sampled cell's draw must not depend on which other
cells were sampled or in flight. Rows with identical bound content
share one cell by design (\secref{sec:impl}), and hence one draw; that
sharing is fixed by row content before sampling and leaves
non-interference intact. Certificates are scoped
to the snapshot the oracle draws came from; the cache may be older. A
change of the served model ends the snapshot, and \secref{sec:exp-deploy}
(the September run) measures such a change
(change-of-world maintenance is complementary work).
\end{assumption}
\begin{assumption}[Frozen stratification]\label{as:frozen}
The stratifier is a deterministic function of the cache, the edit, and
row content, fixed before any oracle call. Any such stratifier
preserves validity; its quality affects only power
(\secref{sec:exp-ablations}).
\end{assumption}
\begin{assumption}[Uniform within-stratum sampling]\label{as:sample}
Sampled rows are drawn by a seeded uniform shuffle within each stratum,
without replacement, and each sampled row receives one fresh oracle
draw, independent across rows (Assumption~\ref{as:pin}, which also
covers rows that share a cell). The sampling
itself is fixed by the seeded canonical shuffle of \secref{sec:impl}.
The calibration study of \secref{sec:exp-validity} checks that the
implemented test holds the level Theorem~\ref{thm:valid} states, on
planted strata whose rates sit just above the budget and on the fixed
population that is least favorable to it.
\end{assumption}

\paragraph{No monotonicity shortcuts.} Folk reasoning suggests
one-sided safety: a scope-\emph{widening} edit should preserve TRUE
cells, so cached TRUEs could be reused for free. Our measurements
refute the unconditional form: under a widening edit, \wideningTrueFlip\ of
cached-TRUE cells flipped. The widened definition admits every row the
old one did, so these flips are not the edit's meaning at work. Edit
direction therefore enters \sivm{} only as a
stratification hint; any directional certificate must be
\emph{conditional} on a measured violation rate, never assumed.

\paragraph{The impossibility floor is per-stratum.} Let $s_j$ be
stratum $j$'s self-flip rate: the probability that a fresh draw of a
cell under the \emph{unedited} prompt disagrees with the cached draw,
\emph{among the cells whose cached draw put them in stratum $j$}. For
the identity edit $\pi'{=}\pi$, $p_j$ equals $s_j$ by definition. The
floor concerns certificates that bound a stratum's realized flip count
$M_j$, the count that charges reuse for every disagreement with one
fresh draw. Both modes of \sivm{} issue such certificates. Take any
procedure that certifies a stratum with $M_j > \alpha n_j$ with
probability at most $\delta_j$. For the identity edit at a budget
$\alpha < s_j$ it certifies stratum $j$ with probability at most
\[
  \delta_j + \Pr[M_j \le \alpha n_j]
  \;\le\; \delta_j + \exp\!\bigl(-2\,n_j\,(s_j - \alpha)^2\bigr),
\]
where the second step is Hoeffding's inequality~\cite{hoeffding} for
draws that are independent across cells. (If $w_c$ rows share cell $c$,
the exponent's $n_j$ becomes $n_j^2/\sum_c w_c^2$.) Under the unedited
prompt the certifiable budget is therefore floored by model stability,
up to a term that vanishes as the stratum grows. For a budget just
below the floor that term is not small, and there power, not the floor,
is what limits certification. Three scoping notes apply. First, the
floor limits bounds on the whole count. A rule that bounds only the
presented-cells rate can go below it by sampling more, because sampled
cells are recomputed; in the limit it samples the whole stratum and
reuses nothing. Second, the conditioning is not incidental. Because
membership is decided by one draw, a minority-value stratum is
selected partly \emph{for} having flipped that way, so $s_j$ is a
property of the (cache, stratifier) pair, not of the column.
Building the cache by a majority vote over several draws at write time
lowers it (\secref{sec:exp-ablations}).
Third, the floor is a bound for the identity edit only. An edit that
sharpens the output distribution, or shifts it toward a stratum's
cached value, can drive $p_j$ below $s_j$, and the
model study contains such strata (\secref{sec:exp-families}). For every
other edit the floor is a reference point, not a bound.

The floors are unequal, and the column-level average describes
neither stratum. We measure each floor as defined, the stored cached
value against three fresh draws of the same prompt per row. On our
Boolean column the
cached-FALSE stratum has $s{=}$\floorFalseStratum\ (95\% row-cluster
bootstrap \floorFalseCi) while the cached-TRUE stratum has
$s{=}$\floorTrueStratum\ (\floorTrueCi), against \floorIdentityPooled\
pooled. Rows are bucketed by the stored cached
value the certifier itself stratifies on, and the interval resamples
rows, since draws from one row are dependent. The column-level
\floorBool\ quoted elsewhere is the rate between pairs of fresh draws.
Three otherwise
separate observations follow from this single measurement. The FALSE
stratum is the only one that certifies at all, and it is certifiable in
principle down to budgets near its own floor. At $\alpha{=}0.05$ it
certifies in \edgeCertRate\ of replications, some only at the deepest
look of the schedule, for \edgeSavingsMean\ mean savings
(Table~\ref{tab:main}).
There, power rather than the floor is what binds. The TRUE stratum is
never certified at any budget we tried: its estimated floor is above
the loosest of them, though the interval reaches below it. Its size
sets the corpus-scale savings
ceiling: with \deployTrueStratum\ of \deployN\ cells cached TRUE, reuse
cannot exceed \deployCeiling. The ceiling belongs to \emph{this} cache.
A cache built by majority vote at write time lowers the stratum's floor
but not its flip rate under the formatting edit
(\secref{sec:exp-ablations}). And any method that reuses cells from the
cached-TRUE stratum inherits that stratum's flip rate no matter what
aggregate target it
was given, which is what we observe when we run one
(\secref{sec:exp-baselines}, B2$'$). Column-level stability is the wrong
unit; the certifier's stratification recovers the right one, and a
standing refusal on a stratum is a stability diagnosis of that slice of
the column.

\section{The \sivm{} procedure}
\label{sec:system}

Given the edit $\pi\to\pi'$ and cached cells for the affected rows
(\figref{fig:procedure}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Diff.} Compute a word-level longest-common-subsequence
(LCS) diff of the prompts; collect content terms of added/removed spans
(the \emph{delta}). The delta is recorded in the certificate for
provenance and consumed only by the optional refinement in step~2; it
never enters the reuse decision itself.
\item \textbf{Stratify (frozen).} Partition rows by cached value and
freeze the partition before any oracle call
(Assumption~\ref{as:frozen}). With a binary proxy this step is the
stratification of proxy-certified selection~\cite{supg}, transported
from one-shot selection to maintenance. What changes is the guarantee
it carries: a bound per stratum instead of one aggregate bound over
the selected set (\secref{sec:exp-baselines}, B2$'$, isolates exactly
that difference). Optionally refine each value stratum by an
\emph{interaction score}, a measure of how strongly the delta matches
the row (lexical delta-term overlap, or the embedding cosine between the
delta text and the row's bound content), bucketed into tertiles. This
refinement leaves validity intact
(Assumption~\ref{as:frozen}). It pays only when
within-value flip risk is heterogeneous, and otherwise costs power
through the $\delta/K$ split (\secref{sec:exp-ablations}).
\item \textbf{Schedule.} For stratum $j$ of size $n_j$, fix a doubling
look schedule $L_j = (45, 90, 180, \dots)$ capped at $n_j$, with at most
six looks. Split the stratum's failure probability $\delta_j = \delta/K$
evenly across looks. Every experiment here pins $n_0{=}45$, at most six
looks, and $\delta{=}0.1$. Here $K$ is the number of cached values: 2 on the
Boolean columns and 4 on the select column. In the free-text pilot of
\secref{sec:limits} and Appendix~\ref{app:pilot} there are 8 category
strata. A look on a large
Boolean stratum is therefore tested at the per-look level
$\delta' = \delta_j/|L_j| = 0.1/12$, and a look on a large select
stratum at $0.1/24$. A small stratum has fewer looks and a
correspondingly larger per-look level.
\item \textbf{Sample and test.} Order the stratum's rows canonically,
shuffle them once with the seed (Assumption~\ref{as:sample}), and at
each look $t$ reveal one fresh draw for each row of the prefix
(Assumption~\ref{as:pin}). Compute the exact finite-population upper
confidence bound $u_{j,t}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact} on the stratum flip
rate at level $\delta_j/|L_j|$; \emph{certify and stop} if
$u_{j,t} \le \alpha$; \emph{stop for futility} if the empirical rate
alone exceeds $\alpha$.
\item \textbf{Apply.} Reuse all unsampled rows of certified strata
(recording certificate provenance per cell); recompute the rest.
Sampled rows are already fresh.
\end{enumerate}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:sivm} states the procedure in full. What the test
conditions on is fixed before the first oracle call: the
stratification, the look schedule with its per-look levels, the seed,
the bound, and the equivalence relation. The budget $\alpha$ and the
mode enter only where a look's bound is compared with a threshold. The
event on which every scheduled look's bound holds does not depend on
them, so several budgets, and both modes, can be read from one sample
path at no cost in $\delta$ (\secref{sec:exp-deploy} reads three
budgets). Two results in this article were computed after their draws
had been seen, and are marked as retrospective where they appear: the
replay of the August draws under the exact bound
(\secref{sec:exp-deploy}) and the graded relation of
\secref{sec:exp-main}. The guarantee is per certification. Each
certified edit spends its own $\delta$, so over $m$ certifications the
chance that at least one certificate is wrong is at most $m\delta$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\sivm{} certification of an edit $\pi \to \pi'$}
\label{alg:sivm}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE cached cells $\{(r_i, c_i)\}$; edit $\pi \to \pi'$; budget
$\alpha$; failure probability $\delta$; first look $n_0$; at most $\ell$ looks;
mode $\in \{\text{default}, \text{strict}\}$;
Assumptions~\ref{as:pin}--\ref{as:sample}
\ENSURE reuse set $\mathcal{R}$, recompute set, certificate record
\STATE $\Delta \leftarrow$ word-level LCS diff of $\pi$ and $\pi'$
\STATE strata $1, \dots, K \leftarrow$ partition of the cells by cached
value, optionally refined by an interaction bucket of $\Delta$; freeze
\FOR{each stratum $j$ with $n_j$ cells}
  \STATE $\mathrm{order}_j \leftarrow$ seeded shuffle of the stratum's
  rows in canonical order; $L_j \leftarrow (n_0, 2n_0, \dots)$ capped at
  $n_j$, at most $\ell$ looks; $\delta' \leftarrow \delta / (K\,|L_j|)$
  \FOR{each look $n \in L_j$}
    \STATE draw $X_i'$ for the first $n$ rows of $\mathrm{order}_j$ not
    yet drawn; $k \leftarrow |\{ i \le n : c_i \not\equiv X_i' \}|$
    \STATE $u \leftarrow$ exact upper bound of Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact} at
    $(k, n, n_j, \delta')$
    \STATE $\alpha' \leftarrow \alpha$ in the default mode, else
    $\alpha (n_j - n)/n_j$
    \IF{$n < n_j$ \AND $u \le \alpha'$}
      \STATE certify stratum $j$; \textbf{break}
    \ENDIF
    \IF{$k / n > \alpha$}
      \STATE refuse stratum $j$ (futility); \textbf{break}
    \ENDIF
  \ENDFOR
  \STATE refuse stratum $j$ if its schedule is exhausted
\ENDFOR
\STATE $\mathcal{R} \leftarrow$ unsampled cells of certified strata;
recompute set $\leftarrow$ unsampled cells of refused strata
\STATE persist the certificate (budget, $\Delta$, per-stratum evidence)
and apply $\mathcal{R}$ additively
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\keeprunin
\paragraph{The bound.} Fix a stratum of $N$ cells ($N = n_j$), and give
every cell one hypothetical fresh draw under $V'$, the coupling on which
the presented-cells estimand is defined. Let $M$ be the number of cells
whose fresh draw flips. The sample of $n$ cells is drawn uniformly
without replacement and independently of those draws, and shows $k$
flips. Conditional on the $N$ outcomes, $k$ is
$\mathrm{Hypergeometric}(N, M, n)$, and
$\Pr[\mathrm{Hyp}(N, M, n) \le k]$ is non-increasing in $M$. The exact upper confidence bound at level
$\delta'$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact}
  u \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\,\max\bigl\{\, M : \Pr[\mathrm{Hyp}(N, M, n) \le k]
  > \delta' \,\bigr\},
\end{equation}
the finite-population form of the Clopper--Pearson
bound~\cite{clopper}, to which it tends as $N$ grows. Because the
coverage holds conditionally on every population realization, it holds
unconditionally over sampling and draw randomness together, and it
bounds the realized flip count $M$ rather than its expectation. This is
the natural bound for a binary loss under the sampling the procedure
performs. On a clean sample its cost is $\ln(1/\delta')/n$ to first
order. The empirical-Bernstein bounds pay range terms with constants of
$7/3$ (Maurer--Pontil, on $\ln(2/\delta')$) and $7/3 + 3/\sqrt{2}$
(Bardenet--Maillard, on $\ln(5/\delta')$) that a binary loss does not
need. On a clean sample of $n{=}90$ from a stratum of
\boundExactAtNStratum\ cells at the pinned per-look level, the exact
bound reads \boundExactAtN. In the \deployFalseStratum-cell deployment
stratum it reads \boundExactAtNLarge; there it coincides with the
binomial limit to three digits. On the clean sample of 90 from
\boundExactAtNStratum\ cells, the Maurer--Pontil
bound~\cite{maurer} reads \boundEbAtN, and the
Bardenet--Maillard without-replacement
bound~\cite{bardenet} reads \boundWorAtN. \secref{sec:exp-ablations}
and Appendix~\ref{app:grid} measure the difference on every
configuration and price a betting
confidence sequence~\cite{wsr}; time-uniform
boundaries~\cite{howard} are discussed in \secref{sec:limits} as the
remaining lever.
The system exposes the alternatives as ablation arms.

\begin{theorem}[Validity under peeking]\label{thm:valid}
Let $K$ be the number of strata produced by the frozen stratifier,
$\delta_j = \delta/K$, and let every look use the exact bound of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact} at level $\delta_j/|L_j|$. Under
Assumptions~\ref{as:pin}--\ref{as:sample}, with probability at least
$1-\delta$ over sampling and oracle draws, every certified stratum $j$
has realized whole-stratum flip count $M_j \le \alpha n_j$;
consequently $\mathrm{FR}^{\mathrm{pres}}_j \le \alpha$ for every
certified stratum and for every union of certified strata (the empty
certification is vacuously valid). In the strict (reuse-set) mode, where
certification at sample size $m_j$ requires
$u_{j,t} \le \alpha\,(n_j{-}m_j)/n_j$, the same argument gives
$M_j \le \alpha (n_j - m_j)$, hence
$\mathrm{FR}^{\mathrm{reuse}}_j \le \alpha$. The statements hold for any
per-look bound that is a valid upper confidence bound at that level for
uniform without-replacement draws, including the Bardenet--Maillard
bound~\cite{bardenet}; the Maurer--Pontil bound~\cite{maurer} is stated
for independent draws and is retained only as an ablation arm.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix stratum $j$ and condition on its $n_j$ fresh-draw outcomes, of
which $M_j$ flip. If $M_j > \alpha n_j$, certifying at look $t$ requires
$u_{j,t} \le \alpha < M_j/n_j$, i.e., the observed count to fall in the
lower tail that Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact} rejects at level $\delta_j/|L_j|$;
by the monotonicity of the hypergeometric tail in $M$, that event has
probability at most $\delta_j/|L_j|$ over the sampling, for every
population realization, hence unconditionally. Because the looks are
prefixes of one shuffle fixed before sampling, the prefix of size $n_t$
(the $t$-th entry of $L_j$) is, marginally, a uniform
without-replacement sample of that size, whether or not the procedure
reaches look $t$. The bound therefore fails at any one scheduled look
with probability at most $\delta_j/|L_j|$, and a union over all
scheduled looks covers whichever look the data stops at. That
union bound over the
$|L_j|$ looks and the $K$ strata gives $\delta$; this is the Bonferroni
instance of Learn then Test~\cite{ltt}, each (stratum, look) pair
testing the null $M_j > \alpha n_j$ with a certificate as the
rejection. The futility rule only stops sampling, never certifies, so
it cannot increase the error probability. On the good event,
$\mathrm{FR}^{\mathrm{pres}}_j = (M_j - k_j)/n_j \le \alpha$, where $k_j$
is the number of flips observed in the sample, and any size-weighted union of
certified strata inherits the bound. In the strict mode the good event
is $M_j \le \alpha (n_j - m_j)$; at worst every flip falls in the
remainder, so $\mathrm{FR}^{\mathrm{reuse}}_j \le \alpha$. The
conditioning uses Assumption~\ref{as:pin} only through
non-interference: the population of fresh-draw outcomes must be the
same whichever cells the shuffle selects.
\end{proof}
\noindent\emph{Remark.} The Bardenet--Maillard bound is proven for the
same uniform without-replacement draws (its Serfling factor shrinks the
variance term as the sampled fraction grows; its constants are those of
Theorem~4.3 of~\cite{bardenet}), so the argument transfers to it
unchanged. The
Maurer--Pontil bound is stated for independent draws. Whether it
remains valid at the sampled fractions our runs reach is not something
we prove, which is why the pinned bound is the exact one. Those fractions
are large: the schedule's deepest look draws \deployAugustFiveLook\
cells of a stratum, most of a benchmark stratum, and the certifier
samples up to \sampleFracHi\ of a benchmark table.

\paragraph{Power.} In a large stratum a clean sample certifies once
$(1-\alpha)^n \le \delta'$, i.e., at $n^{*} = \lceil \ln \delta' /
\ln(1-\alpha) \rceil$. Under the pinned procedure on a Boolean column
($K{=}2$, six looks), this gives \minZeroFlipSample\ at $\alpha{=}0.05$,
against \minZeroFlipSampleEb\ for the Maurer--Pontil bound. The
doubling schedule cannot stop on $n^{*}$: the first look at or above it
is the one it takes. A clean stratum of $n_j{=}5{,}000$ therefore pays
\minZeroFlipRealizedLarge\ draws in calibration. A clean stratum of
$n_j{=}600$ pays \minZeroFlipRealizedSmall, because its
finite-population correction and its shorter schedule's larger per-look
level bring the earlier look under $\alpha$. Either way, savings require strata much
larger than $n^{*}$, i.e., large tables, which is precisely the
deployment regime. Near the frontier, certifying a stratum with true
rate $p$ requires roughly $n \gtrsim z_{\delta'}^2\, p(1-p) /
(\alpha-p)^2$ samples, where $z_{\delta'}$ is the standard normal
quantile at level $\delta'$ (the normal approximation to the exact
test).
This explains the measured collapse of savings as $\alpha \downarrow
p$. On SO formatting (Table~\ref{tab:main}), mean savings are
\frontierA, \frontierB, and \frontierC\ at $\alpha{=}0.2$, $0.1$, and
$0.05$, with refusal at $0.02$.

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:impl}

\sivm{} runs inside \emph{lore}, a working AI-table system. Its
natural-language search bar compiles to a
versioned filter specification executed identically on MySQL and
Elasticsearch (conformance-tested for count, page, sort, aggregation,
and hydration parity on \corpusSO\ rows). AI columns are per-cell jobs
with typed structured outputs, bounded concurrency, retry, and a
content-hash cache keyed on \emph{only the bound fields}
$\beta(\pi)$. Unrelated row changes therefore never invalidate the
cache, and content-duplicate rows share one computation. Every run passes a
pre-run cost gate and lands in a ledger; prompt edits create
immutable versions (the \sivm{} substrate); certificates persist with
full strata evidence, and reused cells carry visible provenance in the
UI. Decode is pinned ($T{=}0$) for all cell computation per
Assumption~\ref{as:pin}. The table's certify action runs the pinned
procedure of \secref{sec:system} on the rows of the user's current
view, at most \routeScopeCap\ per request. The corpus-scale runs of
\secref{sec:exp-deploy} call the same certifier function from a script.

\paragraph{What the user sees.} In the table view a reused cell
carries a shield mark beside its value. Its tooltip shows the
cell's stored rationale with the note that it was reused from the
previous version under a statistical certificate. A cache hit is
annotated as one in the same place. Certifying a column version
returns the certificate's summary to the user: the rows in scope,
$\alpha$, the cells sampled, reused, and to be recomputed, and the
flips observed in the sample. A refused stratum has no mark of its own:
its cells are recomputed and served as ordinary fresh cells, and the
refusal is visible in the certificate record. Whether this surface
changes how a person acts on the column was not studied
(\secref{sec:limits}).

\paragraph{Storage model.} Cells are keyed by
(column, row, prompt version), so versions of a column \emph{coexist}
instead of replacing one another, and a secondary index on
(column, version, model, content hash) serves the cache. This is the one
design decision \sivm{} depends on. Systems that key a cell by
row alone, or that hash the prompt into the cache key, destroy the old
materialization at the moment of the edit. The question this article
asks cannot then be posed: there is nothing left to certify. Keeping
versions side by side turns ``which cached cells still transfer?'' into
a join between two versions of one table, which is why the certifier is
a query over the store, not a service beside it. Two details of that
query matter for reproducibility and for Assumption~\ref{as:pin}. The
certifier orders each stratum's rows canonically (by content hash, then
row identifier) before the seeded shuffle, so the sample path is a
function of the seed and the data alone. An earlier version of the
certifier took the
order the storage engine happened to return. That order changed when
the query planner switched indexes, and the same seed then drew a
different sample (Appendix~\ref{app:deploy}). And a model snapshot is a
version of its own: oracle draws taken after a change of the served model are
stored under a new prompt version carrying the same template. Draws
from two snapshots therefore never share a key and cannot be mixed in
one certification path.

\paragraph{Certificates are records, not decisions.} A certificate
persists the budget $\alpha$, the prompt delta, the per-stratum evidence
(size, cells sampled, bound attained, certified or refused), and the
counts of sampled, reused, and recomputed cells. It is therefore
auditable after the fact: the realized-error audits in
\secref{sec:exp-deploy} are recomputed, from the row identifiers each
run persisted and the stored cells, by a script that imports nothing
from the certifier. Applying a certificate is a
single \texttt{INSERT\,\dots\,SELECT} that carries certified values
forward under the new version and stamps the certificate identifier on
each one.

That insert carries an anti-join against the target version, so reuse is
strictly \emph{additive}: a certificate can supply a value where none
was computed, and can never overwrite one that was. An earlier
implementation used an upsert, which is the obvious way to write it and
is silently destructive. Applying a certificate
after a partial recompute reverted freshly computed cells to the stale
values the certificate had been asked to reuse. The bug is invisible in
aggregate error, because a reverted cell and a reused cell are the same
row with the same value. It surfaces only if something outside the
certifier knows what that cell should have said. Any system that
maintains stochastic cells across versions has this hazard, and the
guard belongs in the write path, not in code review.

Three further rules govern live operation. A sampled cell whose oracle
call fails is counted as a flip and left for recomputation. The rule is
conservative: a failure can add to the flip count the bound sees and
never subtract from it, so it can cost a certificate and cannot create
one. (The studies of \secref{sec:experiments} measure flip rates on
completed draws; Appendix~\ref{app:indep} reports the independence
check under both conventions.) Reuse does not chain. A reused cell is a
copy of the previous version's value, not a draw of the new one, and
the certifier takes only computed cells as its cache. At the next edit
a reused cell is therefore recomputed, and the savings reported here
are per edit (\secref{sec:limits}). Finally, the sampling seed
defaults to a hash of the column and its two versions. Successive
certifications of one column therefore draw different samples, and each
sample is reproducible from the certificate record. The experiments of
this article set the seed explicitly (\secref{sec:exp-setup}).

\paragraph{Scope.} One
budget-class reasoning model carries the full experimental program.
A further \famCount\ models from four other providers, three of
them successive Gemini generations, replicate the lab
column's two pairs\ifnum\famPairsAvailable=1\relax; \famFivePairFamilies\

others do not), so the run dates are the snapshot record. The

\paragraph{Models and decoding.} Every cell in the
primary program uses DeepSeek V4 Flash, called through the Vercel AI
Gateway (gateway identifier \texttt{deepseek/deepseek-v4-flash-0731}).
Those calls were made between \ledgerStart\ and \ledgerEnd, and
again on \sepSnapshotDate\ for the re-certification and the assumption
checks\ifnum\indepExtAvailable=1\relax\ (and on \indepExtDate\ for the
larger concurrent arm of the independence check)\fi.
The model study calls the further models through the same gateway
\famRunDate. Every call passes temperature $0$ and no other decode
parameter. Maximum output length and reasoning effort stay at the
provider's defaults. No decode seed is sent. The seeds of this article
govern row sampling only: one main seed gives the single run reported
per configuration, and \bootB\ further seeds give the sampling
replications. Cells are requested as typed structured
outputs against a JSON schema through the gateway's client library. A call
whose output fails the schema or whose request errors is retried by
the client. A cell that still fails is stored with an error status
and excluded from labels and from every rate in this article. No such
cell exists among the cells written through the column runner; the
probe scripts that call the model directly report their own exclusions
where they occur. The embedding proxy in
baselines B1 and B3 and in the interaction stratifier is
\texttt{openai/text-embedding-3-small}, also through the gateway. Every
labeling pass, sample, and replay is seeded and reproducible from
released scripts.

\paragraph{Labels and spend.} Flip labels are single fresh draws
under the new version, which matches the estimand: a label answers
``would a recompute differ from the cache now?'' Labels are therefore
noisy themselves. A three-draw relabeling of the headline pair measures
by how much. Depending on the instrument, that pair's flip rate reads
between \floorBoolStored\ and \editFlipFreshBoth, and
\labelChurn\ of rows change label between two of the labelings.
Table~\ref{tab:rates} reconciles each flip-rate figure quoted for the
headline pair on the evaluation vector, and for its cached-TRUE
stratum, with the edit, the cells, and the instrument behind it. It is
population functionals, not labels, that the guarantee addresses. This
is the same picture the LLM-as-annotator literature draws, where model
labels reach crowd-worker or expert agreement on many tasks but with
task-specific noise that has to be validated rather than
assumed~\cite{gilardi,ziems,pangakis,tornberg}. Ledgered model spend
across every experiment in this article is \totalSpend\ for
\totalCells\ billed cells, read from the system's own cost ledger.
That figure is a lower bound. A further \offLedgerCells\ cells were

\paragraph{The $\alpha$-frontier and adaptive economics.}
Savings collapse
as $\alpha$ approaches a stratum's flip rate (\figref{fig:frontier}),
as the power analysis predicts; below that rate the certifier refuses.
The doubling schedule prices both directions. A stratum whose sample
rate exceeds the budget is refused at that look, the first one for the
strata farthest from the budget. Near-frontier certifications escalate
sampling only while the data
stays consistent with certifiability. A single refusal near the frontier
is not evidence that the stratum exceeds the budget. The synonym
edit at $\alpha{=}0.1$ is refused on the main seed and certified in
\synTightCertRate\ of the sampling replications, a power miss the
futility rule trades for its early stops.

\paragraph{Noise floors and column stability.} Replicate self-runs
give the two column types' self-flip rates. The Boolean column's floor
is \floorBool, measured between pairs of fresh draws over the
$n{=}\benchN$ rows. The four-way select's floor is \floorSelect\ on a
smaller probe of $n{=}200$, in which every observed self-flip was
between adjacent tiers. At default temperature the Boolean floor reads
\floorDefaultT\ on an $n{=}200$ two-draw probe whose $T{=}0$ arm reads
\floorTzeroProbe. The default-temperature floor alone exceeds a $5\%$
budget, which motivates decode pinning. A formatting-only edit flips
somewhat more cells than no edit does: on the Boolean column,
\editFlipFreshBoth\ against \floorBool\ with fresh draws on both
sides. On these two columns the flip rate tracks the column's own floor
more closely than the edit's class: the Djinni criteria change lands at
\flipDjCriteria, indistinguishable from the formatting edit at
\flipDjFormatting. Five author-written pairs cannot say how general
that is.

\paragraph{Certificates that exceed the budget.} A valid procedure
may issue wrong certificates, in at most a $\delta$ share of runs.
Table~\ref{tab:main} contains one configuration where it does:
\tableUnsafeWorstPair\ at $\alpha{=}\tableUnsafeWorstAlpha$, whose
certified stratum flips at a rate just above the budget. It certifies
in \tableUnsafeWorstCert\ of replications. Every one of those
certificates is unsafe in the sense of Theorem~\ref{thm:valid}, and
that share sits inside the per-stratum $\delta_j = \delta/K = 0.05$ of
that column. This is an exact test spending part of its $\delta_j$ on a
stratum just past the budget, the behavior the calibration study
measures at scale (\secref{sec:exp-validity}). The reuse-set rate,
which the default mode does not certify, exceeds $\alpha$ on some
default-mode certificates in \tableExceedConfigs\ configurations (the
exceed.\ column of Table~\ref{tab:main}).

\paragraph{The strict mode.} The strict mode guarantees the reused
cells alone, at a threshold that shrinks as sampling deepens. That
stronger guarantee is nearly free where a stratum's flip rate is well
below the budget and expensive near it. On the headline edit it costs
nothing at $\alpha{=}0.2$ and two points of mean savings at
$\alpha{=}0.1$ (\reuseSetSavingsTight\ against \frontierB). At
$\alpha{=}0.05$ it certifies in \reuseSetCertEdge\ of replications,
where the default mode certifies in \edgeCertRate. On the select column
it cuts mean savings by more than a third at $\alpha{=}0.2$ and
certifies in at most \reuseSetDjCertTightMax\ of replications at
$\alpha{=}0.1$. Appendix~\ref{app:strict} tabulates both modes at the
three budgets where either certifies.

\paragraph{A graded relation (retrospective).} Exact match is the
strictest relation a select column can use, and the relation is the
user's to state (\secref{sec:limits}). Every self-flip observed on the
seniority column was between adjacent tiers. After the exact-match
results were in, we therefore re-scored the stored labels of that
column's formatting edit under a tolerance: a cell flips only when the
fresh tier is more than one step from the cached one. Under the
tolerance the edit flips \gradedFlipsDjFmt\ of \benchN\ cells, where
exact match flips \flipDjFormatting. The pinned procedure then
certifies it in every replication at $\alpha{=}0.05$, a budget that
exact match refuses, for \gradedSavingsEdgeDjFmt\ mean savings. This is
a weaker guarantee, not a tighter one. It bounds moves of two tiers or
more and says nothing about moves of one tier. The certifier is
unchanged: Theorem~\ref{thm:valid} needs only a fixed binary flip
indicator, which need not come from a transitive relation. The
tolerance would be the wrong relation for the column's criteria edit,
whose stated purpose is to move some candidates up one tier. Of that
edit's \gradedCritExactFlips\ exact-match flips,
\gradedCritOneTier\ are one-tier moves, and a tolerance would certify
away the change the user asked for.

\subsection{Baselines}
\label{sec:exp-baselines}

Four comparisons isolate what \sivm{} adds. Table~\ref{tab:policies}
lines the policies up, and Appendix~\ref{app:baselines} gives the
detail of each.

\emph{(B1) Verified-cache similarity has no cross-version signal.} The
per-row similarity between the v1-bound and v2-bound prompts, the
quantity a verified semantic cache thresholds, predicts cell flips at
chance. The area under the receiver-operating-characteristic curve
(AUC) is \aucVcacheF\ on the formatting edit and \aucVcacheW\ on the
widening edit. Adding ``a version key'' to such a cache therefore
cannot produce a sound cross-version policy. Our interaction score
carries signal when the edit is semantic (AUC \aucInterW\ on widening)
and none when flips are noise-driven (\aucInterF\ on formatting). That
is why the cached \emph{value} carries the stratification in the
formatting case.

\emph{(B2) Aggregate-only certification}, the pinned procedure without
strata, is valid for its aggregate estimand and silently corrupts
subgroups. At $\alpha{=}0.2$ on the formatting edit it certifies
\aggFsavings\ of the cells at \aggFrealized\ aggregate error, while
the cached-TRUE cells it reuses carry \aggFsubgroup\ error. Per-stratum
bounds are what make the guarantee mean something for every
user-visible slice of the table.

\emph{(B2$'$) A SUPG-style competitor, matched.} The closest existing
method is proxy-certified selection~\cite{supg}: score rows with a cheap
proxy, spend an oracle budget labeling a sample, and threshold the
proxy so that the selected set's error clears the target. We
transported it to maintenance with the proxy the reduction in
\secref{sec:system} names, the cached value itself. We matched it to
\sivm{} on the oracle calls, the labels, the error target, the bound,
and the total failure probability $\delta$. Matched this way it wins on
volume and loses on where its errors land. At $\alpha{=}0.2$ it reuses
nearly every cell on both edits and satisfies the aggregate bound it
was given. The extra volume is the volatile stratum: \hhSupgTrueRows\
cached-TRUE cells on the formatting edit, from a stratum whose floor is
\floorTrueStratum. \sivm{} reuses none of them. An aggregate target is
satisfied by any mixture that averages out, so it buys volume with the
correctness of the least stable subgroup. The result does not depend on
the choice of proxy, nor on the competitor's looser per-test level
(Appendix~\ref{app:baselines}).

\emph{(B3) Guarantee-free embedding transfer}
(GPTCache-style~\cite{gptcache}, $\tau{=}0.95$), the deployed status
quo, reuses every cell on the formatting edit and \bThreeReuseW\ of
them on the widening edit, absorbing error at about the population flip
rate. Its threshold controls volume, never error. A sweep of $\tau$
takes the reused share from nearly everything to nothing within a
window about $0.03$ wide, while the error among reused cells stays at the population
flip rate (Appendix~\ref{app:baselines}). The trivial policies, always
recompute and always reuse, bracket the design space in
Table~\ref{tab:policies}. \sivm{} is the only row whose reused cells
carry a per-stratum bound.

\subsection{Other models}
\label{sec:exp-families}

Every measurement above uses one model. We therefore ran the lab
column's two pairs on \famCount\ further budget-class models from four
other providers (Table~\ref{tab:fam})\ifnum\famPairsAvailable=1\relax,
and the remaining three pairs on \famPairsFamilies\ of them
(Table~\ref{tab:fampairs})\fi. The runs use the same seeded rows as the
primary evaluation vector, the same prompts, $T{=}0$, and one fresh draw
per cell. Each model's cache is its own draw under the old version, and
its floor a second draw of that version. A flip is therefore the model
disagreeing with itself across the edit, never a comparison with the
primary model. The two tables report the replication analysis of
Table~\ref{tab:main}, repeated on each model's stored draws.

\paragraph{What transfers.} Every model has a measurable self-flip
floor on the lab Boolean column, from \famFloorLo\ to \famFloorHi\
(Table~\ref{tab:fam}). On the production column one model,
\famZeroFloorModel, shows no self-flip in its rows
(Table~\ref{tab:fampairs}).
Certified reuse is not a property of the primary model. On each of the
\famCount\ further models at least one edit certifies in at least
\famRepBestMin\ of replications at the loose budget. And certificates
stay inside their budgets. Across the \famRepCells\ cells of the two
tables, a certificate whose realized presented-cells error exceeds its
budget occurs in at most \famRepUnsafeMax\ of a cell's replications.

\paragraph{What does not transfer.} Which edits are benign does not
transfer. The formatting-only edit is the benign one on the primary
model. It certifies in every replication on Gemini~3 Flash and
Gemini~3.8 Flash, and in almost none on Gemini~2.5 Flash-Lite and
GPT-5 nano. On GLM-4.7-Flash it certifies only a small stratum
(\famGlmFmtRepSavings\ mean savings). On Gemini~2.5 Flash-Lite the edit
flips \famGeminiFmt\ of cells against a floor of \famGeminiFloor.
Decomposed, its two paragraph breaks alone flip \famGeminiSpaceOnlyFlip\
and its one-word case change alone \famGeminiCaseOnlyFlip. The edit
also moves the share of TRUE answers on that model, from
\famGeminiTrueShareOld\ to \famGeminiTrueShareNew. On GLM-4.7-Flash it moves that share the other
way, from \famGlmTrueShareOld\ to \famGlmTrueShareNew. Self-disagreement
alone flips cells both ways and leaves the share where it was, so on
these two models the edit shifts the model's decision. On the three
models where the formatting edit fails, the synonym rewording certifies
a stratum in most replications instead, because its flips concentrate
in one cached value and leave the other reusable. The ordering that the
edits' labels suggest (formatting benign, rewording not) therefore
inverts on some models and holds on others. Benignness is a property of
the (edit, model) pair, and the certifier measures it per stratum
without consulting the label.

\paragraph{Floors are per stratum.} The floor binds for the identity
edit only (\secref{sec:problem}), and two strata of this study flip
less under an edit than under none. Under the formatting edit
GLM-4.7-Flash's cached-FALSE stratum flips \famGlmFmtFalseStratum\
against an identity floor of \famGlmFloorFalseStratum, and GPT-5 nano's
cached-TRUE stratum flips \famNanoFmtTrueStratum\ against
\famNanoFloorTrueStratum. GPT-5 nano's pooled floor, \famNanoFloor,
sits above every budget in the grid. Its one certifying stratum on the
lab column sits below that pooled figure for the same reason: a floor
is a per-stratum quantity, measured under the first version's prompt.

\subsection{Deployment scale}
\label{sec:exp-deploy}

To run the certifier at the scale of a full corpus, we materialized
the Boolean lab column across all \deployN\ rows. ``Deployment scale''
means that scale throughout: a script calls the certifier the table
uses, and no outside users are involved. The materialization came to
\deployMatCells\ billed
cells in a single ledgered run costing \deployMatCost, which took
\deployMatSpanHours\ hours of wall clock at \deployConcurrency-way
concurrency. The remaining \deployFreeRows\ rows cost nothing, served
by content-hash caching or already materialized by earlier passes. We
then certified the formatting edit against that column, with the real
model as oracle, at three budgets ($\alpha{=}0.2$, $0.1$, and $0.05$)
on two model snapshots over one seeded sample path
(Table~\ref{tab:deploy}). The first certification uses the oracle draws
of the live run of August 2026. The second was drawn live on
\sepSnapshotDate, \driftDaysLater\ days later, against the same cache.
The budgets share the path at no cost in $\delta$: the per-look bounds
do not depend on $\alpha$, so the event on which they all hold covers
each budget. The August run itself certified under the Maurer--Pontil
bound, which Theorem~\ref{thm:valid} does not cover, and the system
applied that certificate. Table~\ref{tab:deploy} therefore replays the
August draws under the exact bound. Those rows are retrospective: the
bound was fixed after the draws had been seen. The September rows were
drawn live under the exact bound, fixed beforehand.
Appendix~\ref{app:deploy} gives the certificate the system applied in
August.

\paragraph{The August draws, replayed.} At the loose budget the exact
bound certifies the cached-FALSE stratum at look \deployLookLoose. The
whole certification therefore costs \textbf{\deployOracle\ oracle
calls} and reuses \textbf{\deployReused\ cells}, \deploySavings\ of
the column. The ceiling is \deployCeiling, set by the cached-TRUE
stratum the certifier never certifies. The procedure thus captures
essentially all the reuse available to capture, and the residue is a
property of this cache (\secref{sec:problem}). The
tight budget, $\alpha{=}0.1$, costs \deployOracleTight\ calls. At
$\alpha{=}0.05$ the stored draws run out before the schedule's deepest
look. Drawing the missing cells later would mix two snapshots, so the
August evidence is silent there. Audited against the oracle-computed
cells of that snapshot, the reused rows flip at \deployRealized\
(\deployAuditFlips\ of \deployOverlap\ audited rows). A separate
verifier script recomputes every audit of Table~\ref{tab:deploy} from
the row identifiers each run persisted and the stored cells.

\paragraph{The September run: drift, measured.} Assumption~\ref{as:pin}
scopes a certificate to the model snapshot its oracle draws came from,
so we re-ran the certification live on \sepSnapshotDate: same model
identifier, same gateway, same prompts, same cache, same seeded path.
The snapshot had moved. On the \driftFalseRows\ cached-FALSE rows the
August run had sampled, the edit's flip rate against the cache rose
from \driftFalseAugEdit\ to \driftFalseSepEdit\ (McNemar
$p\driftFalseMcNemarP$). We cannot tell a change of the provider's
model from a change of its default settings or of how the gateway
routes the same identifier; to the user they are one event. The
certifier measures the sum of these effects against the cache it is
asked to reuse, which is the quantity a user is exposed to, and its
certificates moved accordingly. The loose budget
certified again at look \sepLookLoose, for \sepOracleLoose\ calls, in
\sepWallLoose\ seconds of wall clock including the oracle calls. The
tight budget needed \sepOracleTight\ calls, one look deeper than on the
August draws. The budget $\alpha{=}0.05$ was refused by the futility
rule after \sepOracleFive\ calls. The refusal is correct: the
stratum's flip rate against the aged cache exceeds that budget, so its
cells are recomputed rather than served.
Audited against every September oracle cell, the reused rows flip at
\sepRealizedLoose\ under the loose certificate and \sepRealizedTight\
under the tight one, inside both budgets and well above the August
figure. Appendix~\ref{app:deploy} gives the like-for-like drift
comparison, the per-look evidence, and the run's accounting.

\paragraph{Sample cost against table size.} The schedule stops on
evidence about a stratum's \emph{rate}, and its deepest look is fixed.
The sample is therefore bounded whatever the table's size, while the
population a certificate covers grows with the table. At
$\alpha{=}0.2$ the certification spent \deployOracle\ calls on
\deployN\ rows, where the benchmark's replications spend
\benchOracleRange\ calls (mean \benchOracleMean) on \benchN\ rows.
Savings therefore rose from \benchSavingsMain\ on the benchmark's main
seed to \deploySavings\ at the scale of the corpus. Savings here are
shares of rows; rows with identical bound content share one stored cell
(Appendix~\ref{app:deploy}).

\paragraph{Calibration.} The calibration study plants strata at
known flip rates and runs the certifier on them (\figref{fig:power}).
It covers \calConfigs\ configurations: planted rates $p \in
\calPRange$, budgets $\alpha \in \{\calAlphaGrid\}$, three stratum
sizes, and both modes, \calTrials\ runs in all. The quantity
Theorem~\ref{thm:valid} bounds is the rate of unsafe certificates,
certifications of a stratum whose realized flip count exceeds the
budget. Its worst value over all configurations is \calUnsafeWorst,
against the per-stratum $\delta_j{=}\calNominalDelta$ the study plants.
Planting by rate is not the least favorable case, because the realized
count of a planted stratum often falls on the safe side of the budget.
The least favorable case is a fixed population whose flip count sits
one flip past the budget, and there the probability of an unsafe
certificate can be computed exactly. Over the \calBoundaryCells\
(budget, size) combinations of the grid it is at most \calBoundaryHi\
in the default mode and \calBoundaryStrictHi\ in the strict mode,
inside $\delta_j$ in each. The gap to $\delta_j$ comes from the
Bonferroni split across looks, from the discreteness of the test, and
from the futility rule, which stops sample paths that a later look
might have certified. Appendix~\ref{app:cal} gives the computation, the
argument that this population is the least favorable one, and the
value for each combination.

\ifnum\indepAvailable=1\relax
\paragraph{Independence across rows.} Assumption~\ref{as:pin} asks
that oracle draws not interfere across rows, and provider-side batching
could in principle correlate concurrent requests~\cite{yuan,tml}. We
tested it on \indepN\ rows of the evaluation vector. The formatting
edit's new version was drawn twice for every row against the same
cache, once with a single request in flight and once at a concurrency
of \indepConcurrency. A further concurrent arm of \indepExtRows\ rows
was drawn a day later. Appendix~\ref{app:indep} gives every test; three
findings matter here.

First, among completed requests the flip rates agree: \indepSeqRate\
sequential against \indepConcRate\ concurrent. That comparison could
detect a difference of \indepMdd\ percentage points, not a smaller
one. The lag-one tests of serial dependence do not reject in any arm.

Second, concurrency tripled the requests that failed or timed out,
from \indepSeqErrors\ to \indepConcErrors\ of \indepN. The certifier
counts a failed call as a flip (\secref{sec:impl}). Counted that way
the two arms read \indepSeqFailRate\ and \indepConcFailRate\
($p{=}\indepFailP$). The rule is conservative, so under load this costs
certificates, not validity.

Third, dependence among draws issued together is not excluded. Over
blocks of \indepBlockSize\ completions, flip counts are more dispersed
than independent draws would make them. Pooled over both concurrent
arms, their variance is \indepPooledDispersion\ times the binomial
value (95\% interval \indepPooledDispersionCi), and the one-sided test
rejects narrowly. A simulation prices that for the least favorable case, a
stratum whose flip rate sits one flip past the budget. With independent
draws the certifier errs there with probability at most
\overdispIndep. At the pooled estimate it errs with probability
\overdispPooled, and at the upper end of the interval with probability
\overdispPooledUpper, against a nominal $\delta_j$ of $0.05$. The first
concurrent arm alone, eight blocks drawn under slow serving, is
compatible with much stronger dependence.

The check therefore supports less than independence. It does not show
that concurrent draws interfere. It also cannot rule out an error
probability two to three times the nominal one on a stratum that sits
just past its budget. Three things limit that exposure. It is confined
to such strata, and at $\alpha{=}0.2$ the deployment stratum's audited
error is far below its budget. The replication results of this article
resample frozen labels, a fixed population for which the hypergeometric
law holds however the labels were drawn, so only a live certification
relies on the assumption. And a certification sample is small, so its
calls can be issued one at a time at a cost in wall clock and none in
calls; the sequential arm is too short to show that no dependence
remains then. The check covers one model through one gateway at one
concurrency, in the September snapshot. The September certificates of
\secref{sec:exp-deploy} were themselves drawn concurrently.
\fi

\subsection{Ablations}
\label{sec:exp-ablations}

\paragraph{The bound ablation.} Every result above spends its $\delta$
through the exact bound of Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact}, split across looks by
Bonferroni. The bound, more than the stratification or the schedule,
sets the frontier. We measured how much by swapping it inside the same
pinned procedure over the same stored labels (Table~\ref{tab:bounds};
Appendix~\ref{app:grid} gives every configuration). Five bounds are
compared: the exact bound; its binomial limit, the Clopper--Pearson
bound~\cite{clopper}; the Maurer--Pontil empirical-Bernstein
bound~\cite{maurer}; the Bardenet--Maillard without-replacement
bound~\cite{serfling,bardenet}; and a Waudby-Smith--Ramdas betting
confidence sequence~\cite{wsr}, which needs no split across looks.

Across the \boundConfigsTotal\ configurations of
Table~\ref{tab:main} (main seed), the exact bound certifies
\boundExactConfigs\ of them, against \boundEbConfigs\ under
Maurer--Pontil and \boundWorConfigs\ under Bardenet--Maillard, at
higher mean savings and on fewer oracle calls. The empirical-Bernstein
bounds pay a range term that a binary loss does not need. The betting
sequence beats the exact bound on \boundBetBetter\ of the
\boundConfigsTotal\ configurations, where the split across looks bites
hardest, and loses to it on \boundBetWorse. Its validity for draws without replacement rests on a
caveat that Appendix~\ref{app:grid} states. All five arms were also
replayed through the calibration protocol. In that replay the exact
bound's rate of unsafe certificates at planted nulls is at most
\boundExactNullUnsafe\ against a per-stratum $\delta_j$ of
\boundNullDeltaS. The exact bound is the pinned one because it is the
bound the sampling model itself implies: it uses the hypergeometric law
with no relaxation, where the empirical-Bernstein bounds relax it to
cover losses a binary flip cannot take.

\paragraph{The stratification hierarchy.} One stratum is too few:
aggregate-only certification conceals the cached-TRUE subgroup's error
(baseline B2). More strata than the cached values are usually too many.
Refining each value stratum by embedding-interaction tertiles keeps
validity (Assumption~\ref{as:frozen}) and costs power through the
$\delta/K$ split, unless within-value risk is heterogeneous. On the
headline configuration the refinement certifies less than value strata
alone (\ablationSavings\ against \benchSavings\ mean savings on SO
formatting at $\alpha{=}0.2$). Across the \ablationTotal\ shared
configurations of the released ablation it is better in
\ablationBetter\ and worse in \ablationWorse. Every win sits at a
budget where value strata alone certify in at most
\ablationWinBaseCertMax\ of replications. We report this as a negative
result. On these columns within-value risk is homogeneous enough that
the split is repaid only where the value strata certify almost nothing.

\paragraph{Minority-stratum anatomy.} The cached-TRUE stratum of the
Boolean column flips \minoritySingle\ under a formatting edit. One
candidate cause is cache noise, which a majority vote of three draws at
write time would remove~\cite{selfconsistency}. The stored draws do not
support it. Voting on both sides (vote-of-3) brought the stratum's flip
rate to \minorityVote\ in a dedicated probe and to \minorityVoteRerun\
on the three-draw labels, two separate runs over the same
\votedStoredTrueN\ rows. The vote overturned \minorityNoise\ and
\minorityNoiseRerun\ of the cached TRUE values, respectively. A cache
built by the vote fares no better. Its TRUE stratum, the \votedTrueN\
rows whose three-draw majority is TRUE, has a lower floor
(\votedTrueFloor\ against \floorTrueStratum) but flips at
\votedTrueEditFlip\ against fresh draws of the edited prompt
(Table~\ref{tab:rates}). Voting at write time cleans the cache without
making the minority stratum certifiable under this edit. Refusing and
recomputing that stratum is the response the data support.

\section{The prompt-edit benchmark}

task~\cite{sembench}; none exercises maintenance across prompt versions,

against the new version. Ours comprises five components. The first is
two redistributable corpora: the Stack Overflow Developer Survey
(\corpusSO\ rows, ODbL~1.0 with the Database Contents License,

\paragraph{Guaranteed semantic query processing.} Semantic-operator
systems bound the error of \emph{one-shot} execution against a
reference model. They include LOTUS's optimized operators with accuracy
targets~\cite{lotus}, SUPG's proxy-based selection with precision/recall
guarantees~\cite{supg}, and its aggregation counterpart ABae, which
stratifies by proxy before spending an expensive predicate~\cite{abae}.
They also include BARGAIN's adaptive cascade thresholds~\cite{bargain},
ThalamusDB's approximate natural-language predicates with error
bounds~\cite{thalamusdb}, and pipeline-level accuracy budgeting in Task
Cascades~\cite{taskcascades}. All certify a \emph{fixed}
definition per query; none transfers evidence across a definition
change. We reuse their statistical framing (a labeled sample, a bound, a
threshold) in a maintenance loop those systems do not model, with a
different bound. The loss is binary and the sample is drawn without
replacement from a frozen stratum. The exact finite-population bound,
the hypergeometric form of Clopper and Pearson's binomial
limits~\cite{clopper}, is therefore available, and it dominates the
empirical-Bernstein bounds that a general bounded loss calls for
(\secref{sec:exp-ablations}). Time-uniform confidence
sequences~\cite{howard,wsr} are the alternative that would remove the
split across looks. The certificate itself is distribution-free risk
control in the sense of risk-controlling prediction sets~\cite{rcps} and
Learn then Test~\cite{ltt}. It is an upper confidence bound on a risk
that must clear a user-set $\alpha$ with probability $1-\delta$, here
evaluated per stratum and per look with the multiple-testing accounting
those frameworks make explicit. The declarative LLM data systems that
optimize cost and quality without a statistical target are Palimpzest's
AI-powered analytics~\cite{palimpzest}, DocETL's agentic pipeline
rewriting~\cite{docetl}, Evaporate's structured views over data
lakes~\cite{evaporate}, and UQE's sampling-driven queries over
unstructured collections~\cite{uqe}. They compute the same kind of
prompt-defined per-record outputs. None certifies which of its outputs
survive a redefinition.

\paragraph{Sampling inspection and sequential tests.} Statistically, a
certification is a lot-acceptance decision. A stratum is a finite lot, a
flip is a defect, and the exact bound is the hypergeometric calculation
of acceptance sampling by attributes~\cite{dodgeromig}, with the
tolerated fraction set by the user and the decision spread over looks.
The look schedule is a group-sequential design. We split the failure
probability evenly across the looks, where the spending functions of
that literature~\cite{pocock,landemets} would allocate it unevenly.
Risk-limiting election audits~\cite{rla} share the shape of the whole
procedure: sample until the evidence bounds the risk of a wrong
outcome, and otherwise fall back to the full count, as a refusal falls
back to recomputation.

\paragraph{Verified caching.} vCache certifies that a \emph{new query}
may reuse a cached answer under one fixed definition, with learned
per-entry thresholds and user error rates~\cite{vcache}. Evergreen
spends confidence sequences and early stopping to cut the cost of
\emph{verifying} semantic aggregates, not to decide
reuse~\cite{evergreen}. FreshCache approves a cache hit only when a
learned staleness model puts the probability that the entry is stale
below a per-tier error budget~\cite{freshcache}. FreshCache is the
closest in shape, since a per-partition budget gating reuse is exactly
our skeleton, and it differs on the two axes that decide the guarantee.
Its risk is change-of-\emph{world} (open-web evidence aging under fixed
query semantics) where ours is change-of-\emph{definition}. Its per-tier
quantity is a fitted model's point estimate where ours is a
finite-sample upper confidence bound that either clears $\alpha$ or
refuses. Our B1 baseline
shows why these mechanisms do not port: across a definition change the
query-to-entry similarity that such caches threshold carries no
per-cell flip signal (AUC \aucVcacheF\ and \aucVcacheW). \sivm{} certifies
change-of-\emph{definition} transfer, the axis those systems hold
fixed; the two compose (a certificate is scoped to a snapshot, and
change-of-world machinery decides when the snapshot itself expires).

\paragraph{Incremental and adaptive view maintenance.} Sampling a stale
materialized view to bound the error of answers served from
it~\cite{svc} is the closest classical relative of what we do, and the
difference is what goes stale. There the data drifts under a fixed view
definition; here the definition itself changes over fixed data. That is
why that work's estimators condition on updates and ours on a prompt
delta. The look schedule itself is online aggregation's
template~\cite{ola}, a running estimate whose interval tightens as
sampling proceeds, repurposed to certify a stratum rather than to answer
an aggregate. Classical incremental view maintenance (IVM) propagates
base-data changes through a fixed view definition~\cite{dbsp}, and view
adaptation after redefinition~\cite{gupta95} rewrites the
materialization when the definition changes. Both assume deterministic
operators, where a rewrite either preserves a subexpression or does not.
Stochastic LLM cells break the premise. Our measurements show that even
a semantically null rewrite flips cells at about the column's noise
floor (\flipSoFormatting\ against \floorIdentityPooled, both measured
against the stored cache), so ``preserved'' is not a
property a rewrite can have here, only a rate one can bound. Streaming
semantic systems recompile operators mid-stream but recompute outputs
instead of certifying reuse of old ones~\cite{vectraflow,contprompts}.
Streaming cascades certify on the \emph{data-arrival} axis, where new
tuples meet a fixed operator, and leave the definition axis
open~\cite{streamcascades}.
Given that distinction,
\sivm{} is, to our knowledge, the first
maintenance procedure whose unit of transfer is a
\emph{statistically certified set of stochastic cells}.

The nearest counterexample in shape is step-level reuse with lightweight
verification and selective patching across perturbed
requests~\cite{stepcache}, which does reuse computation when a request
changes. It differs on exactly the property this article is about: its
verification is a deterministic per-step check, so it carries no error
budget and makes no population statement about the set it reuses.
In-database prompt management~\cite{promptdb} and versioned,
provenance-tracked prompt views~\cite{spear} supply the substrate this
work assumes, not a competing mechanism. We take the framing of prompts
as versioned views from that line and claim only the certification
machinery built on top of it, whose reuse is of \emph{outputs}, not of
a model's internal state.

\paragraph{Prompt sensitivity and regression.} Prompt-delta analysis
for assertion synthesis~\cite{spade} anticipates our edit taxonomy but
targets pipeline quality checks, not reuse certification; interactive
LLM regression tools frame migration pain without
guarantees~\cite{retain}. Our finding that surface edit class can fail
to predict flip rate (a synonym paraphrase flipping more cells than a
formatting edit; a formatting edit indistinguishable from a criteria
change) shows why measurement, not taxonomy, must carry the decision.
Formatting changes that preserve meaning are known to move task
accuracy widely, and differently across models~\cite{sclar}. Our
per-cell flip rates, and their variation across models, are that
sensitivity seen at the level of individual outputs. A served model can
also change under a fixed name~\cite{chendrift}, which is the drift our
September re-certification measured against a cache.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Which budgets are usable, and for what.}
Table~\ref{tab:budgets} sets out what each budget tolerates and what it
buys. Most of the savings sit at $\alpha{=}0.2$ and $\alpha{=}0.1$, and
neither is a small tolerance. A budget is a share of a certified
stratum. On the running example the certified stratum is the
cached-FALSE one, so the cells at risk are rows shown as FALSE that a
recompute would show as TRUE: hidden positives. At the scale of the
corpus the loose budget tolerates \budgetCellsLoose\ of them, about
twice the \deployTrueStratum\ rows the column marks TRUE at all. The
tight budget still tolerates more than that number. The audits found
fewer, but not few. Carried to all the reused cells, their error rates
come to about \deployHiddenLoose\ hidden positives on the August draws
and about \sepHiddenLoose\ in September. Each is a row on which a
recompute would differ, not a row known to be wrong, and a certificate
promises only its budget.

A budget that loose is defensible where a missed positive is cheap and
the column is advisory: a lead list or an enrichment sheet that a
person keeps working through, or an exploratory filter that is
recomputed before anything is decided on it. The alternative on offer
there is today's status quo, which reuses everything at the population
flip rate with no bound at all. The same budget is not defensible where
a FALSE closes a case (a screen that rejects on it), or where the
column feeds an aggregate or a downstream filter. There a tighter
budget and the strict mode are the right setting, and
Table~\ref{tab:budgets} shows what they buy. At $\alpha{=}0.05$ the
stable Boolean column still certifies in most replications, but for
less than half the savings, and the strict mode certifies almost never.
The less stable select column certifies nothing below $\alpha{=}0.1$,
and little at it. At the scale of the corpus nothing below
$\alpha{=}0.1$ certified: the August draws stop short of the look that
$\alpha{=}0.05$ needs, and the September run refused it. Tight budgets
are affordable in calls, because the sample is bounded: \sepOracleTight\
calls at $\alpha{=}0.1$ in September, against \deployN\ cells. What
they cannot buy is certification of a stratum whose flip rate is near
or above them. A practitioner should therefore read the frontier of
\figref{fig:frontier} against the column's floor, and choose the
budget the downstream use tolerates, not the one that maximizes
savings.

\paragraph{Statistical scope.} Bonferroni across strata and looks is
sound but conservative, and \secref{sec:exp-ablations} prices it. The
exact bound leaves a gap between its measured unsafe-certificate rate
and the nominal $\delta$. A betting confidence sequence, which needs no
split across looks, beats it where that split
bites hardest and matches or loses to it elsewhere. The
without-replacement betting sequence of~\cite{wsr} or a time-uniform
boundary~\cite{howard} would keep the exact bound's validity under
without-replacement sampling while removing the split. A
group-sequential calibration of the six looks~\cite{pocock,landemets}
is a third route. The finite sum behind the calibration study's
worst-case figures gives the exact error probability of any per-look
levels, so the levels could be chosen to spend nearly all of
$\delta_j$, up to the discreteness of the test. Levels tuned to one
budget would give up the reading of several budgets from one sample
path. The default mode is also
conservative for its own estimand. The presented-cells rate leaves out
the $k_j$ flips the sample observed, so certifying when
$u_{j,t}\,n_j - k_j \le \alpha n_j$ would bound it as well. We keep the
simpler rule, which bounds the whole-stratum count too.
E-value selection (the e-value Benjamini--Hochberg
procedure)~\cite{ebh} composes with any of these. None of them is
needed for the guarantees stated here. The independence of oracle draws
across rows is an assumption that our check probes but does not
establish (\secref{sec:exp-validity}); a live certification under
concurrency inherits whatever dependence the serving path introduces.
Our stratifiers are first instances. The lexical variant is weak on
broad edits, and the embedding variant's signal exists only for
semantic edits. Learned interaction models, or a cheaper model's answer
used as a proxy, are therefore an open lever with guaranteed validity
(Assumption~\ref{as:frozen}).

\paragraph{Sequences of edits.} Reuse is one edit deep in the released
system. A cell reused under a certificate is recomputed at the next
edit (\secref{sec:impl}), so savings do not accumulate over a series of
edits. Theorem~\ref{thm:valid} does not force that choice. A later
certification could test the carried-forward values against fresh draws
of the newest version, because the guarantee concerns the stored values
whatever version computed them. We have not evaluated sequences of
edits, and each certification in a sequence spends its own $\delta$.

\paragraph{Equivalence and columns.} In the main evaluation equivalence
is exact match, and the columns are two Boolean columns and one four-way
ordinal select; numeric and free-text columns, which the introduction's
product examples also serve, are not evaluated there. The five
author-written edit pairs, one per class per column, are enough to show
that surface class can fail to predict flip rate, not to estimate how
often it does. The edit-class findings should be read at that strength.
Because both Boolean columns ask one question over one corpus, the
observation that a column's own stability dominates the edit rests on
two columns.

The equivalence relation is the user's to state.
Theorem~\ref{thm:valid} needs only that it yield a binary flip
indicator, so a graded column can take a tolerance in place of exact
match, and \secref{sec:exp-main} measures one on the seniority column.
A numeric column would take a tolerance in the same way; none is
evaluated here. A free-text column needs a
judge, and the judge's own error enters the budget. In a stratum, let $q$
be the share of pairs the judge calls equivalent although a reader would
not (missed flips). The flip rate the judge reports for the stratum is
then at least the true rate minus $q$, and $q$ is at most the judge's
miss rate among the pairs it calls equivalent. So let $\hat{m}$ be an
upper confidence bound on that conditional miss rate at level
$\delta_{\mathrm{cal}}$, from human-labeled pairs of the same column,
and certify at $\alpha - \hat{m}$. A certified stratum's true rate is at
most $\alpha$ whenever the calibration bound and the certificate both
hold, provided the judge's conditional miss rate in the certified
stratum is no higher than in the calibration pool. They both hold with
probability at least $1 - \delta - \delta_{\mathrm{cal}}$. Errors in the
other direction (calling equivalent cells different) only raise the
reported rate and cost power. The calibration
belongs to the (edit, column) pair it was drawn from, or to a pool of
comparisons on one column if their conditional miss rates are taken as
equal, since a judge's miss rate is a property of the outputs it
compares. The machinery of this article is otherwise unchanged, but its
power under a noisy judge \ifnum\pilotAvailable=1\relax has one measurement so far\else is an open measurement\fi.
\ifnum\pilotAvailable=1\relax

\paragraph{Models and snapshots.} One budget-class reasoning model
carries the full experimental program. A further \famCount\ models were

moved, i.e., composing \sivm{} with change-of-world

\paragraph{Economics.} The economic frame that matters in production is
time-to-consistency under provider rate limits. In that frame certified
maintenance samples \sampleFracLo--\sampleFracHi\ of cells across the
configurations of Table~\ref{tab:main} that certify in most replications
(\sampleFracLo\ on the headline formatting edit at $\alpha{=}0.2$)
and recomputes only the strata it refuses, instead of recomputing
$100\%$. That is a wall-clock and rate-limit gain
that grows with table size, because sample sizes do not. The live
re-certification of \secref{sec:exp-deploy} measured the certification
decision at \sepWallLoose\ seconds of wall clock for the loose budget.
This article does not measure two things. The certificate surface in the UI was
not studied with users, so whether a person acts differently on a
column that shows one is open. No claim here depends on the answer:
the guarantee is a statement about the cells, whatever the interface
shows, and a user study is human-subjects work of its own. And the cost
figures come from one ledgered materialization and one priced
maintenance replay at the gateway's list rates. Each further live
measurement would cost a full recomputation of the corpus, so the
figures show the scale of the saving, not a cost model.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Prompt-defined columns are materialized views whose \emph{definition}
changes far more often than their data, and the systems serving them
today choose between recomputing everything and reusing everything.
\sivm{} shows the middle is reachable and cheap: freeze a
stratification, sample on a doubling schedule, bound each stratum's flip
rate with an exact finite-population bound, reuse only what clears the
budget. At the scale of a full corpus and a budget of $\alpha{=}0.2$,
that cost \deployOracle\ oracle calls to certify reuse of
\deployReused\ of \deployN\ cells. The sample did not
have to grow with the table, because the schedule stops on evidence
about a \emph{rate}, at a cost bounded independently of the size. These
results come from one primary model on Boolean and select columns. On
\famCertCount\ of \famCount\ further models, from four providers, at
least one edit certified, and which edits did so varied by model.

The result we did not anticipate is that the certifier doubles as a
measuring instrument for the column it maintains. A stratum refused at
every budget is a slice of the table on which the model does not
reliably reproduce its own answers, identified by a futility stop at
the cost of one look.
A flip rate that has risen on the same rows against the same cache
\driftDaysLater\ days later is a model that has moved under it. The
certifier reports the movement in the currency the user pays, oracle
calls per certified budget. Certified maintenance is
therefore useful even where it certifies nothing: the refusal
identifies, cheaply, reuse error that would otherwise go unmeasured.

\section{The mechanical guards}
\label{app:guards}
The checker (\texttt{scripts/check-paper-numbers.ts}) fails on any of
ten conditions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A hand-typed quantity in the prose outside an enumerated
allow-list (user parameters such as $\alpha$ and $\delta$, protocol
constants such as the schedule looks, and generic thresholds quoted as
such).
\item A quantity hardcoded inside the generator instead of derived from
an artifact.
\item A macro defined but never cited, since an uncited macro can drift
undetected.
\item A macro whose trailing space is swallowed into the next word,
which is invisible in the source.
\item A sentence that asserts two quantities differ while citing two
macros whose values are equal.
\item A spelled-out multiplier (``twice'', ``an order of magnitude'')
that the cited macros' ratio does not support.
\item A containment claim (``inside'', ``at most'') that the cited
range and scalar macros do not support.
\item An absolute claim (``never'', ``every'', ``anywhere'') appearing
in the abstract or the contributions list without an entry in an
enumerated allow-list. This is the one class of defect the numeric
guards are blind to by construction.
\item A scope claim (``on the same column'') whose cited macros come
from different columns, checked against a provenance file the generator
emits from the same pair definitions the experiments run on.
\item A sentence of the prose, of a list item, of a caption, or of the
abstract longer than 60 words (table bodies, displayed mathematics, and
the theorem environments excluded), a readability bound.
\end{enumerate}
The generator adds guards of its own. It asserts the relations that
\secref{sec:experiments}, \secref{sec:limits}, and the appendices state
between figures: in the paragraphs on the main results, the strict
mode, the stratifier ablation, the matched competitor, the model study,
the independence check, the usable budgets, the graded relation, and
the pilot. It also asserts that the separate audit verifier reproduces
each audit the article prints, and that the released certify action is
configured as the pinned procedure. It stops if a regenerated artifact
breaks one. It rounds every figure half up on its decimal value, and
rounds upward a figure quoted as a maximum (``at most'').
Generating the quantities is necessary but not sufficient, because the
sentences that \emph{relate} quantities go stale independently of the
quantities themselves.

\section{The bound ablation in detail}
\label{app:grid}
\paragraph{The bounds compared.} Over the \boundConfigsTotal\
configurations of Table~\ref{tab:main} (main seed), the exact bound's
mean savings are \boundExactSavings\ against \boundEbSavings\ under
Maurer--Pontil and \boundWorSavings\ under Bardenet--Maillard, on
\boundExactOracle\ oracle calls against \boundEbOracle\ and
\boundWorOracle. Relative to
the exact bound, Maurer--Pontil loses \boundEbLost\ certificates
and gains \boundEbExtra, and of the shared ones it reaches
\boundEbSameLook\ at the same look and \boundEbLater\ at a later one.
The widest single gain is \boundGapPair\ at $\alpha{=}\boundGapAlpha$,
from \boundGapEbSavings\ savings for \boundGapEbOracle\ calls
to \boundGapExactSavings\ for \boundGapExactOracle. Of the two
empirical-Bernstein bounds, the without-replacement form pays more,
not less, because its constants are larger. The preprint
reported it as tighter. Its replay used the Maurer--Pontil linear
constant inside the Serfling form, and the published constant,
$\kappa = 7/3 + 3/\sqrt{2}$~\cite{bardenet}, is what the numbers here
use. The Clopper--Pearson limit certifies \boundCpConfigs\ configurations
at mean savings \boundCpSavings\ on \boundCpOracle\ calls, the
finite-population correction buying the exact bound its one further
certificate. The betting sequence certifies
\boundBetConfigs\ configurations at mean savings \boundBetSavings\ on
\boundBetOracle\ calls. It beats the exact bound on \boundBetBetter\ of
the \boundConfigsTotal\ configurations, where the schedule's
Bonferroni split bites hardest (\boundBetOverExactPair\ at
$\alpha{=}\boundBetOverExactAlpha$: \boundBetOverExactSavings\ against
\boundBetOverExactExactSavings). It loses to it on \boundBetWorse\ and
matches it on the rest (certificates
lost \boundBetLost, gained \boundBetExtra). Its validity here rests
on a capital process built for draws whose conditional mean is
constant, which without-replacement draws do not have. The
without-replacement variant given in~\cite{wsr}, which we did not
implement, or a time-uniform boundary~\cite{howard} would remove
the split without that caveat and is the remaining power lever we see.

Validity comes before power, so all five arms were replayed through
the calibration protocol, in a run separate from and smaller than the
calibration study of \secref{sec:exp-validity} (\boundCalTrials\ trials
per arm, \boundNullTrials\ of them at planted rates above the budget). At
planted nulls the exact bound certifies up to \boundExactNullWorst\ of
trials, against a per-stratum $\delta_j$ of \boundNullDeltaS. Most
of those certificates are correct, because the realized count of
a planted stratum falls below $\alpha n_j$ in many trials. The
certificates whose realized count exceeds the budget, the event of
Theorem~\ref{thm:valid}, are at most \boundExactNullUnsafe\ of trials
in any null configuration ($\alpha{=}\boundExactNullUnsafeAlpha$,
$p{=}\boundExactNullUnsafeP$, $n_j{=}\boundExactNullUnsafeSize$; Wilson
\boundExactNullUnsafeCi). The certification rate at a planted null is
not the theorem's event, and it may exceed $\delta_j$ without a
violation. In \boundExactNullsOver\ of the \boundNullConfigs\ null
configurations does its interval lie wholly above $\delta_j$. The Clopper--Pearson limit certifies \boundCpNullWorst\ of
null trials at worst (\boundCpNullUnsafe\ unsafe), and the betting
sequence \boundBetNullWorst\ (\boundBetNullUnsafe\ unsafe).
Maurer--Pontil and Bardenet--Maillard certify
\boundEbNullWorst\ and \boundWorNullWorst\ (\boundEbNullUnsafe\ and
\boundWorNullUnsafe\ unsafe), leaving almost all of their $\delta_j$
unspent. A clean \boundCleanStratum-cell stratum at
$\alpha{=}0.05$ costs the exact bound \boundExactCleanSample\ draws
(\minZeroFlipRealizedLarge\ at $n_j{=}\minZeroFlipLargeSize$
in the calibration study). That figure is a tie: at exactly that size
the bound on a clean look of that many draws equals the budget,
and a stratum a few cells larger pays the next look. It costs the Clopper--Pearson limit
\boundCpCleanSample, the sequence \boundBetCleanSample, Maurer--Pontil
\boundEbCleanSample, and Bardenet--Maillard \boundWorCleanSample.
The frontier in \figref{fig:frontier} is therefore a property
of the bound.

\paragraph{The check.} Each of the \indepN\ rows had the formatting
edit's new version drawn once with a single request in flight and once
at a concurrency of \indepConcurrency\ (\secref{sec:exp-validity}). The
sequential arm took \indepSeqMinutes\ minutes of wall clock and the
concurrent arm \indepConcMinutes. The planned comparison excludes
requests that errored or exceeded a \indepCallCeiling-second ceiling,
and the ceiling binds more often under concurrency. The released
certifier does not exclude them: it counts a failed call as a flip.
Under that rule the sequential arm reads \indepSeqFailCount\ flips of
\indepN\ and the concurrent arm \indepConcFailCount\ (two-proportion
$p{=}\indepFailP$). Of the rows, \indepFailOnlySeq\ count as flips only
sequentially and \indepFailOnlyConc\ only concurrently (exact McNemar
$p{=}\indepFailPairedP$). A certifier under concurrent load therefore
sees more flips than one that issues its calls one at a time, which
makes it refuse more often and never certify more. The larger arm,
drawn under faster serving, had no failed request. On completed
requests the two arms' rates give a two-proportion $p{=}\indepDiffP$.
On the \indepBothRows\ rows both arms kept,
the rates are \indepBothSeqRate\ sequential and \indepBothConcRate\
concurrent. Of those rows, \indepOnlySeqFlips\ flipped only sequentially
and \indepOnlyConcFlips\ only concurrently (exact McNemar
$p{=}\indepPairedP$). The excluded rows show no sign of being
flip-prone, on small counts. Of the \indepConcDroppedRows\ rows the
concurrent arm dropped, \indepConcDroppedSeqFlips\ flipped in the
sequential arm. Of the \indepSeqDroppedRows\ the sequential arm
dropped, \indepSeqDroppedConcFlips\ flipped in the concurrent arm.

\paragraph{The planned tests.} The script fixed three analyses before
the run, each reported against its reference distribution at the
\indepLevel\ level. The first is a two-proportion test on the flip
rates, whose minimum detectable difference at \indepPowerTarget\ power
is \indepMdd\ percentage points. The second sets the two arms'
disagreement on the same row against the August stratum floors. The
third looks for serial dependence in completion order, with a lag-one
permutation test and a dispersion test of flip counts over consecutive
blocks of \indepBlockSize\ completions. The two arms disagree with each
other on
\indepAgreeDisagree\ of rows (\indepAgreeCi): \indepAgreeFalse\ of
\indepAgreeFalseN\ cached-FALSE rows and \indepAgreeTrue\ of
\indepAgreeTrueN\ cached-TRUE rows, against August floors of
\floorFalseStratum\ and \floorTrueStratum. After the drift measured
in \secref{sec:exp-deploy}, that second comparison cannot separate concurrency from the
change of snapshot, and we draw nothing from it.
Table~\ref{tab:indep} gives the third analysis. In the concurrent arm
the dispersion ratio's 95\% confidence interval (CI) is
\indepConcDispersionCi, which covers

\paragraph{Reading the tests.} The rate and lag tests do not reject.
The dispersion test rejects one-sided at the \indepLevel\ level in the
concurrent arm, on \indepBlocks\ blocks.
To add blocks we drew a concurrent arm alone on a further
\indepExtRows\ rows of the vector. Its block
dispersion ratio has a 95\% CI of \indepExtDispersionCi, and its flip
rate set against the sequential arm's gives $p{=}\indepExtVsSeqP$.
This arm was
drawn a day later, on other rows, and under faster serving (a median
response of \indepExtMedianLatency\ seconds against
\indepConcMedianLatency\ in the first concurrent arm), so the
comparisons between arms also span a day of the snapshot. Pooled over
both concurrent arms, the
dispersion statistic is \indepPooledChi\ on \indepPooledDf\ degrees of
freedom ($p{=}\indepPooledP$), a ratio of \indepPooledDispersion\
with a 95\% CI of \indepPooledDispersionCi. The block-scale question
is therefore open. The larger arm alone does not reject. The pooled
test, which includes the first arm, rejects narrowly. The sequential
arm's ratio does not reject either, on \indepSeqBlocks\ blocks, which
are too few to show that one-at-a-time draws are free of dependence. A
block holds about three expected flips, where the chi-square reference
is approximate. Permutation p-values for the same statistics, from
shuffles of each arm's own flip sequence, differ from the chi-square
ones by at most \indepPermGap\ and fall on the same side of the
\indepLevel\ level in every case.

Two looks that were not in the plan argue for caution. Flips
concentrate in slow responses: in the larger arm, \indepExtSlowFlips\
of the \indepExtHalfRows\ slower responses flip against
\indepExtFastFlips\ of the \indepExtHalfRows\ faster ones. Completion
order is therefore not neutral with respect to the outcome. With blocks
formed in launch order instead, the dispersion ratio is
\indepExtLaunchDispersion\ in the larger arm ($p{=}\indepExtLaunchP$)
and \indepLaunchDispersion\ in the first ($p{=}\indepLaunchP$). The
ordering was chosen after seeing the data, so we report these as
cautions, not as findings.

\section{The calibration study in detail}
\label{app:cal}
\paragraph{The planted study.} The study of \secref{sec:exp-validity}
plants flip rates $p \in \calPRange$ at stratum sizes
$\{600, 1{,}800, 5{,}000\}$ and budgets $\alpha \in \{\calAlphaGrid\}$,
in both modes, with $2{,}000$ trials per configuration. The worst rate
of unsafe certificates, \calUnsafeWorst\ (95\% CI \calUnsafeWorstCi),
occurs at $\alpha{=}\calUnsafeWorstAlpha$, $p{=}\calUnsafeWorstP$,
$n_j{=}\calUnsafeWorstSize$, in the \calUnsafeWorstEstimand\ mode. The
presented-cells rate, which leaves out the sampled flips, exceeds
$\alpha$ in at most \calUnsafePresentedWorst\ of runs. Of the
configurations, \calTightNulls\ are planted just above the budget, at
$1.05$ to $1.25$ times $\alpha$, where the futility rule often cannot
dispose of the stratum at the first look and the sampling fraction
climbs. Over these the unsafe rate averages \calUnsafeTightMean. Their
certification rate reaches \calTightCertWorst, and most of those
certificates are correct. At $p$ just above $\alpha$ the realized count
of a planted stratum falls at or below the budget in a large share of
trials, and the exact bound certifies what is actually there. The
reuse-set rate evaluated on default-mode certifications fluctuates past
$\alpha$ in frontier configurations. The worst is \calWorstReuseMetric\
of certified runs at $p{=}\calWorstP$, $\alpha{=}\calWorstAlpha$,
certified \calWorstCertRate\ of the time. That is expected, since the
default mode does not certify that metric. The strict mode holds its own
guarantee within $\delta_j$, with unsafe certificates in at most
\calUnsafeStrictWorst\ of runs.

\paragraph{The least favorable population.} Fix a stratum of $N$ cells
of which $M$ flip. Under the pinned schedule the certifier's decision at
each look is a threshold on the flips seen so far. It certifies if the
count is at most the largest count whose bound clears the budget (a look
that takes the whole stratum cannot certify). It stops for futility if
the sample's rate exceeds the budget, and otherwise it goes on. The
flips among each look's new cells are hypergeometric given the flips
already seen. The probability of certifying is therefore a finite sum
over the paths of cumulative counts, which the released script
evaluates exactly, with the thresholds of the released certifier.

That probability does not increase with $M$. Take two populations that
differ in one cell, a flip in the larger and none in the smaller, and
sample both by one shuffle. At every look the larger population's sample
holds at least as many flips. A path of counts that certifies still
certifies when every count is lowered: lower counts pass every
threshold the path passed and trip no futility stop that it did not.
Every shuffle that certifies the larger population therefore certifies
the smaller one. In the default mode a certificate is unsafe exactly
when $M > \alpha N$, so its probability is largest at
$M = \lfloor \alpha N \rfloor + 1$, one flip past the budget. In the
strict mode the unsafe event depends on the look, and the script finds
the worst $M$ by scanning. Table~\ref{tab:boundary} gives the result
for each combination of the grid. The script also cross-checks one
combination per budget by running the released certifier on shuffled
populations.

\section{The baselines in detail}
\label{app:baselines}
\paragraph{B1 and B2.} On the formatting edit the similarity score's
entire range is \simLo--\simHi, because shared row text dominates. The
aggregate-only arm runs the same schedule and budgets as the pinned
procedure. On the formatting edit it \aggTightCertF\ at $\alpha{=}0.1$
(\aggFtSavings\ savings, \aggFtRealized\ aggregate error) while the
reused cached-TRUE subgroup carries \aggFtSubgroup\ error. On the
widening edit it \aggTightCertW\ at $\alpha{=}0.1$ and certifies
\aggWsavings\ at $\alpha{=}0.2$ with \aggWsubgroup\ subgroup error. The
effect is not an artifact of giving the baseline a shorter schedule.

\paragraph{B2$'$: how the competitor was matched.} We matched the
competitor to \sivm{} on every axis we could: the same number of oracle
calls, identical labels, identical error target, the same exact bound
on the selected population, the same total failure probability
$\delta$, and the same reported quantity. Each method's $\delta$ is
split across the number of tests its own procedure performs: \sivm{}
across the Boolean column's two strata and six looks, $\hhSivmPerTest$
per test; the competitor across its candidate thresholds,
$\hhValuePerTest$. The reported quantity is the error among reused
cells, not the presented-cells rate \sivm{} certifies. Run instead on
each stratum separately at $\delta/K$ with a single look, the
competitor would be the non-adaptive special case of \sivm{} itself.

At $\alpha{=}0.2$ on the formatting edit the competitor reused
\hhSupgReused\ cells to \sivm{}'s \hhSivmReused\ from the same
\hhBudget\ labels. At \hhSupgErr\ realized error it satisfies the
bound it was given, against \sivm{}'s \hhSivmErr. On the widening edit
the same pattern holds: \hhSupgReusedW\ reuses at \hhSupgErrW\ versus
\hhSivmReusedW\ at \hhSivmErrW. The extra volume is drawn from the
volatile stratum, \hhSupgTrueRows\ cached-TRUE cells on the formatting
edit and \hhSupgTrueRowsW\ on the widening edit. With the cached value
as the proxy there are only three candidate thresholds, and at
$\alpha{=}0.2$ the aggregate bound clears at the loosest one. The arm
therefore selects \hhSupgSelFrac\ of the rows it was allowed to on the
formatting edit and \hhSupgSelFracW\ on the widening edit. Matched this
way, the competitor reduces to B3, reuse everything, with a certificate
attached. Its bound is satisfied, its aggregate error is real, and its
behavior is indistinguishable from the guarantee-free status quo.

\paragraph{B2$'$: two caveats.} First, the cached value is the proxy
the reduction names, and the result does not depend on that choice. Run
instead with an embedding-interaction proxy, and with its much larger
threshold search corrected accordingly, the competitor certifies in
\hhEmbCertCells\ of the \hhEmbCells\ (edit, budget) configurations and
lands in the same place. At $\alpha{=}0.2$ on the formatting edit it
reuses \hhEmbReused\ cells at \hhEmbErr\ error, \hhEmbTrueRows\ of them
cached-TRUE cells that flip at \hhEmbTrueErr. On the widening edit it
reuses \hhEmbReusedW\ at \hhEmbErrW, \hhEmbTrueRowsW\ of them
cached-TRUE cells at \hhEmbTrueErrW. The proxy changes how much the
competitor reuses, not where its errors land. Second, with the
cached-value proxy the competitor's per-test level is looser than
\sivm{}'s, because it runs fewer tests. The embedding arm, tested at a
stricter level than \sivm{}'s, still reuses more than \sivm{} at
$\alpha{=}0.2$, so that advantage does not rest on the looser level. The
comparison is matched on the quantity a user sets ($\delta$ in total),
not on the level of any individual test.

\paragraph{B3: the threshold sweep.} The cells the embedding-transfer
baseline reuses at $\tau{=}0.95$ flip at \flipSoFormatting\ on the
formatting edit and at \bThreeErrW\ on the widening edit, where the
population rate is \flipSoWidening. A sweep of $\tau$ over the per-row
similarities shows the baseline collapsing within a threshold window
about $0.03$ wide. On the formatting edit it falls from \tauFmid\ of
rows reused at $\tau{=}0.96$ to \tauFhi\ at $0.98$ and none at $0.99$.
On the widening edit it falls from \tauWmid\ at $0.94$ to \tauWhi\ at
$0.96$ and none at $0.97$. The realized error among reused rows stays
at the population flip rate at every operating point
(\tauFerrLo--\tauFerrHi\ and \tauWerrLo--\tauWerrHi). The threshold
controls volume, never error, because cross-version similarity carries
no per-cell flip signal (B1).
