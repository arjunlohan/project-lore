### Abstract text to paste

Modern tables let users define columns in natural language: a language model computes each cell from a versioned prompt over its row. When the prompt is edited, systems either recompute every cell or reuse stale ones, with unbounded error. We present semantic incremental view maintenance (sIVM), which certifies the cached cells that may be reused after a prompt edit. It groups cells by cached value into strata and bounds each certified stratum's false-reuse rate by a user-chosen error budget with stated confidence. It samples each stratum on a doubling schedule and bounds its flip rate with an exact finite-population bound; a strict mode bounds the error of the reused cells alone. An impossibility floor follows: with the prompt unedited, no stratum certifies below its own rate of self-disagreement. On two public corpora (28,000 labeled cells), a formatting-only edit certifies 88.4% call savings (88.1% mean over 1,000 replications) at an error budget of 0.2 with 3.20% realized false-reuse; at deployment scale, 90 fresh calls certify reuse of 81,424 of 89,184 cells. Across the calibration grid, a certificate exceeds its budget with probability at most 2.6% at the least favorable population, against a nominal 5%. A second certification 55 days later measured drift of the served model and re-certified the same budgets. At least one edit certifies on 6 of 6 further model families; which edits are benign varies by family. The system is released under a source-available noncommercial license, with labels and a benchmark of versioned prompt-edit pairs.
