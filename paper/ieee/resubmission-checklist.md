### Abstract text to paste

In AI tables, a language model computes each cell of a column from a versioned prompt. After a prompt edit, systems recompute every cell or reuse stale ones, with unbounded error. Semantic incremental view maintenance (sIVM) certifies which cached cells may be reused. It groups cells into strata by cached value, recomputes a growing sample of each, and bounds each stratum's flip rate (the share of its cells a recompute would change) with an exact finite-population bound. A stratum is reused when that bound clears the user's budget. The default guarantee covers all cells shown; a strict mode covers the reused cells alone. An impossibility floor follows: with the prompt unedited, no stratum reliably certifies below its own self-disagreement rate. The quantitative results come from one primary model on two public corpora with five author-written edit pairs. On one column, a formatting-only edit certifies reuse of 82.6% of cells on average at budget 0.1. On the full 89,184-row corpus, 90 fresh calls certify reuse of 81,424 cells at budget 0.2, and a live rerun measured model drift. The guarantee assumes independent draws across rows; a check could not rule out dependence among concurrent requests that would make wrong certificates more likely than nominal, and the released certifier now issues its calls one at a time. On six further models (five on all five pairs, one on two), at least one edit certifies, and which edits are benign varies by model. Code and labels are released.
