### Abstract text to paste

Modern tables let users define columns in natural language: a language model computes each cell from a versioned prompt. When it is edited, systems either recompute every cell or reuse stale ones, with unbounded error. We present semantic incremental view maintenance (sIVM), which certifies which cached cells may be reused after a prompt edit. It groups cells by cached value into strata and bounds each certified stratum's false-reuse rate by a user-chosen error budget with stated confidence. It samples each stratum on a doubling schedule and bounds its flip rate with an exact finite-population bound; a strict mode bounds the error of the reused cells alone. An impossibility floor follows: with the prompt unedited, no stratum reliably certifies below its own self-disagreement rate. On two public corpora and one primary model (28,000 labeled cells), a formatting-only edit certifies reuse of 82.6% of cells on average at an error budget of 0.1, and of 88.1% at 0.2 (failure probability 0.1; 1,000 replications). On the full 89,184-row corpus at budget 0.2, 90 fresh calls certify reuse of 81,424 cells, in a live run that also measured the served model's drift over 55 days. On the calibration grid, a certificate exceeds its budget with probability at most 2.7%, against a nominal 5% per stratum. At least one edit certifies on 6 of 6 further models from four providers; which edits are benign varies by model. The system, labels, and five versioned prompt-edit pairs are released under a source-available noncommercial license.
