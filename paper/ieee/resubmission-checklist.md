### Abstract text to paste

Modern tables let users define columns in natural language: a language model computes each cell from a versioned prompt. After a prompt edit, systems recompute every cell or reuse stale ones, with unbounded error. Semantic incremental view maintenance (sIVM) certifies which cached cells may be reused. It groups cells into strata by cached value, recomputes a growing sample of each, and bounds each stratum's flip rate (the share of its cells a recompute would change) with an exact finite-population bound. A stratum is reused when that bound clears the user's error budget; a strict mode bounds the error of the reused cells alone. An impossibility floor follows: with the prompt unedited, no stratum reliably certifies below its own self-disagreement rate. On two public corpora and one primary model (28,000 labeled cells), a formatting-only edit certifies reuse of 82.6% of cells on average at budget 0.1 and 88.1% at 0.2 (failure probability 0.1; 1,000 replications). On the full 89,184-row corpus at budget 0.2, 90 fresh calls certify reuse of 81,424 cells, in a live run that measured the served model's drift over 55 days. On the calibration grid, with independent draws, a certificate exceeding its budget is issued with probability at most 2.7% per stratum, against a nominal 5%. At least one edit certifies on 6 of 6 further models from four providers; which edits are benign varies by model. The system, labels, and five versioned prompt-edit pairs are released under a source-available noncommercial license.
