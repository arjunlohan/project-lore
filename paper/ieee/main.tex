\begin{abstract}
Modern tables let users define columns in natural language: a language
model computes each cell from a versioned prompt. After a prompt edit,
systems recompute every cell or reuse stale ones, with unbounded
error. Semantic incremental view maintenance (\sivm{}) certifies which
cached cells may be reused. It groups cells into strata by cached
value, recomputes a growing sample of each, and bounds each stratum's
flip rate (the share of its cells a recompute would change) with an exact
finite-population bound. A stratum is reused when that bound clears
the user's error budget; a strict mode bounds the error of the
reused cells alone. An impossibility floor follows: with the prompt
unedited, no stratum reliably certifies below its own self-disagreement
rate.
On two public corpora and one primary model (\labeledCells\ labeled
cells), a formatting-only edit certifies reuse of \frontierB\ of cells
on average at budget 0.1 and \benchSavings\ at 0.2 (failure
probability 0.1; \bootB\ replications). On the full \deployN-row corpus
at budget 0.2, \deployOracle\ fresh calls certify reuse of
\deployReused\ cells, in a live run that measured the served
model's drift over \driftDaysLater\ days. On the calibration grid, with
independent draws, a certificate exceeding its budget is issued with
probability at most \calBoundaryHi\ per stratum, against a nominal
\calNominalDelta. At
least one edit certifies on \famCertCount\ of \famCount\ further models
from four providers; which edits are benign varies by model. The
system, labels, and five versioned prompt-edit pairs are released under
a source-available noncommercial license.
\end{abstract}
