\begin{abstract}
Modern tables let users define columns in natural language: a language
model computes each cell from a versioned prompt over its row. When the
prompt is edited, systems either recompute every cell or reuse stale
ones, with unbounded error. We present semantic incremental view
maintenance (\sivm{}), which certifies the cached cells that may be
reused after a prompt edit. It groups cells by cached value into strata
and bounds each certified stratum's false-reuse rate by a user-chosen
error budget with stated confidence. It samples each stratum on a
doubling schedule and bounds its flip rate with an exact
finite-population bound; a strict mode bounds the error of the reused
cells alone. An impossibility floor follows: with the prompt unedited,
no stratum certifies below its own rate of self-disagreement.
On two public corpora (\labeledCells\ labeled cells), a
formatting-only edit certifies \benchSavingsMain\ call savings
(\benchSavings\ mean over \bootB\ replications) at an error budget of
0.2 with \benchRealized\ realized false-reuse; at deployment scale,
\deployOracle\ fresh calls certify reuse of \deployReused\ of \deployN\
cells. Across the calibration grid, a certificate exceeds its budget
with probability at most \calBoundaryHi\ at the least favorable
population, against a nominal \calNominalDelta. A second
certification \driftDaysLater\ days later measured drift of the served
model and re-certified the same budgets. At least one edit certifies on
\famCertCount\ of \famCount\ further model families; which edits
are benign varies by family. The system is released under a
source-available noncommercial license, with labels and a benchmark of
versioned prompt-edit pairs.
\end{abstract}
