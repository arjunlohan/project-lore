Five independent round-2 reviews (statistics/theory, systems/baselines/novelty, economics/deployment, replication/artifact-audit, consistency/provenance), merged and deduplicated against the round-1 list (`draft-review-r1.md`, items 1-22). Every claim below is grounded in at least one reviewer's direct verification against the artifacts in `docs/research/experiments/`; reviewer coverage is tagged per item.

**Counts:** 14 round-1 items or sub-items confirmed resolved · 17 round-1 items still open (14 carry mandatory components, 3 purely nice-to-have) · 8 new findings, all mandatory.

**Votes:** weak reject (B, C, D, E) · conditional weak accept (A). Consensus: **weak reject as submitted, unanimously one focused cycle from acceptance**, with D projecting strong accept next cycle if his four blockers close.

## Consensus verdict

The round-1 reject-level statistical core is genuinely repaired, and this is not taken on faith: the presented-cells vs reuse-set estimand split (r1 item 1) was verified in the theory, in `packages/core/src/sivm.ts` (deflated threshold alpha(n-m)/n, census strata cannot certify), and against `exp8-final-table.json` to the digit; Table 1 is now one pinned procedure generated mechanically, and four of five reviewers independently reconciled all 20 printed rows against the artifact; a 96-configuration known-p calibration and a 12,000-call three-draw relabeling exist where round 1 had nothing. Reviewer D: "more progress in one cycle than most major revisions deliver."

What blocks acceptance is a cluster of self-inflicted, mechanically checkable contradictions between the paper and its own artifacts, three genuinely new empirical problems the revision's own new data surfaced, and the round-1 items that cost API dollars or writing effort, which were left almost untouched. Specifically: (1) the prose still quotes the round-1-condemned mixed-procedure numbers (88.4/72.7/18.7 frontier, 18.7% at alpha=0.05, n*~360) that now contradict the paper's own regenerated table two pages later, flagged by all five reviewers at reject level; (2) the abstract and Table 1 caption flatly deny the 2.0% violation cell the table itself prints, when the estimand-scoped truth is strictly stronger and is the paper's first direct evidence that the guarantee binds; (3) the revision's own mandated experiment (`exp10-labels.json`) supersedes the 3.0% noise floor (fresh floor ~5.0%, CIs exclude 3.0%) and breaks the "2x stability law" (becomes 1.45x), and the draft reports only the one exp10 statistic that leaves the narrative intact; (4) both replication machines run on a broken LCG whose float64 overflow makes shuffles non-uniform and collapses the calibration's effective replication, so no error bar currently means what it says; (5) the mandatory SUPG/BARGAIN baseline still does not exist, and worse, `exp8-supg-baseline.json` is a byte-identical copy of the final table wearing a baseline's name, which will read as fabricated evidence at artifact review; (6) the economics regressed: Table 1 dropped the oracle-calls column, so r2 reports less cost information than the draft round 1 rejected.

Nearly everything on the mandatory list is zero-API-cost (labels are frozen, the calibration is pure simulation, the exp10 draws are on disk); the entire remaining API spend for the minimum acceptance path is under ~$50 (one full-corpus certification run plus an optional expensive-regime datum). None of it requires rethinking the method.

## Part 3: New findings in revision 2 (8, ranked; all mandatory)

### N1. [reject-level (D,E), major (A,B), minor (C) · MANDATORY · 5/5] The paper's central validity claim is contradicted by a number printed inside the table that states it

The abstract (lines 62-64) says realized false-reuse "never exceeded its certified bound"; the Table 1 caption (336-338) says "Violation rates were 0.0% in every certifiable configuration"; Main results repeats it. Two lines below the caption, `paper/table1.tex` prints viol. rate = 2.0% at SO formatting alpha=0.05 (traced: certificationRate=0.204, violationRateReuseSet=0.0196 = exactly 2/102 certifying replications, so the configuration is unambiguously certifiable). The mathematics is fine and the honest statement is strictly stronger: violationRatePresented = 0.000 in all 40 configurations, reuse-set mode is 0 everywhere under its deflated threshold, and the 2.0% is the uncertified reuse-set rate under presented-mode certificates, sitting inside nominal delta=0.1, arising exactly because alpha=0.05 certification only lands at the deep look (1440/1814), leaving a 374-row adversely selected remainder. It is the first direct empirical measurement of the round-1 item-1 selection effect, the only evidence in the paper that the bound is testable rather than merely slack, and the argument for the reuse-set mode's existence. **Fix (all five reviewers converge):** define/relabel the violation column (or split viol_pres / viol_reuse); scope every validity sentence by estimand ("the certified estimand was never violated in any replication of any configuration; the stricter reuse-set criterion was exceeded in 2 of 102 certifying replications at the one marginal configuration"); feature the cell in the text as the adverse-selection signature with the sampling depth that causes it; print 99.6% rather than rounding 0.996 to 100%. Do not delete the cell.

### N3. [major · MANDATORY, zero API cost · D] Both replication machines run on a broken PRNG, so no error bar in the paper currently means what it says

`exp8-final-table.ts` lines 76-86 and `exp9-calibration.ts` lines 22-27 implement s = (1103515245*s + 12345) % 2^31 in JavaScript doubles: the product exceeds 2^53, low bits are silently dropped (after one step every state is divisible by 8).
