Two findings cut the other way and the panel wants them on record, because both help the paper and neither is in it. The panel's summary judgment, reached independently by all five: the science is not the problem and has not been the problem since r6. ### P4. [reject-level · MANDATORY · 5/5, with the direction disputed and adjudicated] The per-stratum floors are estimated with selection on the draws they measure, on a key the certifier does not use, with no interval and no stated join

`scripts/experiments/gen-paper-assets.ts:194-210`; `paper/main.tex:232-256`; `packages/core/src/sivm.ts:174-176`.

The floors are now the paper's central theoretical device and three results are derived from them, so the estimator matters. `gen-paper-assets.ts` assigns each row via `majority(r.d1)` and then measures pairwise disagreement **within that same `d1` triple**. Four defects, in order of how much they move the numbers.

(a) **Circularity.** Conditioning on "$\ge 2$ TRUE" guarantees at least one agreeing pair among the three, so a majority-TRUE row can score at most 2/3 and an all-disagree outcome is impossible. It also excludes the most volatile rows entirely: the `d1` TRUE-count distribution is {0: 1734, 1: 78, 2: 74, 3: 114}, and the 78 single-TRUE rows, which flip at 100% under single-draw conditioning, never enter the TRUE bucket.

(b) **Wrong key.** The paper calls the strata "cached-FALSE" and "cached-TRUE," and `assignStrataWith` strata on a **single stored draw**. Majority-of-three is a different partition: 188 majority-TRUE rows against 178 first-draw-TRUE rows.

(c) **No interval, and the obvious interval is wrong.** No $n$ and no interval appear on any of the three floors. Naive Wilson on the pairs is itself too narrow because pairs are clustered by row: on the published TRUE floor, Wilson gives [22.52%, 29.76%] over 562 pairs while a row-cluster bootstrap over the 266 contributing rows gives **[22.79%, 30.70%]**, and the correct interval on the certifier's own estimand is wider still than the pair count suggests.

(d) **Two floors and the pooled value are computed on different pair sets.** `\floorBool` pools `d1` + `d2` (11,994 pairs) while the stratum floors pool `d1` only (5,996), so "a pooled `\floorBool` that is merely their mixture" holds by luck: the `d1`-only pooled rate is 5.037% and the mixture is 5.0367%, against the printed pooled 4.986%.

The meta-reviewer recomputed six estimators (Part 4, adjudication 1). The published pair is 25.98% / 2.871%. The certifier's own estimand (anchor on each stored draw, score the remaining fresh draws) gives **26.63% / 2.78%**. Fresh-vs-fresh conditioned on the stored draw gives 25.28% / 3.24%. Measuring on the `d2` draws gives 20.2-20.9% / 3.28-3.44% but is measuring the **post-edit** prompt's floor and is confounded by the edit, so it is not a valid alternative.

**One thing that survives every estimator and should be said:** the FALSE stratum's floor upper bound is below $\alpha{=}0.05$ under all six (published [2.46, 3.35], anchor [2.34, 3.23] cluster-robust, leave-one-out [2.52, 4.16]), so the floor-headroom argument at $\alpha{=}0.05$ is robust. It is the point estimate and the interval that are missing, not the conclusion.

*Required fix:* key on the stored v1 cache value (or `d1[0]` failing a join), anchor on that draw and measure disagreement against the draws not used for assignment, which is the estimand the certifier actually faces. **State the join in one clause in the paper**, which r6's close-out required and which was not done. Emit $n$ and a row-clustered interval for all three floors. Compute `\floorBool` on the same pair set as the strata so the mixture identity is exact rather than coincidental.

### P12. [major · MANDATORY · 1/5, meta-reviewer verified numerically] The honest note's causal attribution is refuted by the code that produced it

`paper/main.tex:459-465`; `scripts/experiments/exp13-supg.ts:162-176` versus `:186-197`.

The added note reads: "run with an embedding-interaction proxy instead of the cached value, and with multiplicity correction applied to its threshold search, the competitor certifies nothing at any budget we tried. A proxy that does not separate flipping rows from stable ones cannot be rescued by the selection procedure wrapped around it." The causal claim is refuted by the code.

The two arms are not given the same multiplicity budget: the value arm gets $\delta/3$ (three candidate keep-sets, `:191`); the embedding arm gets $\delta/n_{\text{thresholds}}$ with `nThresholds = sorted.length - 9` (`:162`), i.e. $\delta/126$ at the 135-label budget and $\delta/396$ at the 405-label budget, **42x to 132x tighter**. And their coarsest candidates are the **same sample**: at $k = $ `sorted.length` the embedding arm's $\tau$ is the minimum labelled score, so `sel` is all 135 labelled rows, and the value arm's `keep = [false, true]` candidate is also all 135 labelled rows. Identical rows, identical flips.

The meta-reviewer applied the paper's own `ebUpperBound` to that shared sample (Part 4, adjudication 3). At $\alpha{=}0.2$, $n{=}135$, $k{=}6$: $\delta/3$ gives **0.1667 ≤ 0.2** (certifies, which is why `supgValueProxy.reused` = 1,865) and $\delta/126$ gives **0.2513 > 0.2** (refuses). The embedding arm did not fail because AUC 0.408 carries no signal. **It failed because it was Bonferroni-corrected over 126 thresholds while its twin was corrected over 3.**

*Required fix:* either give the embedding arm a pre-registered coarse grid matching the value arm's cardinality and report what happens, or delete the causal sentence. The re-run is free and the corrected result is **better** for the paper: any aggregate-target selector, informative proxy or not, degenerates to reuse-everything and dumps its error into the volatile stratum. That is the thesis. As written the note draws the opposite lesson from a design artifact, and a referee can refute it from the released JSON in five minutes.

### P14. [minor · MANDATORY · 2/5] Section 3's isolation claim overstates what the experiment holds fixed, and the paper's best positioning sentence is in Section 2 and never carried forward

`paper/main.tex:265-272` (Stratify), `:429-457` (B2$'$).

"What we change is the guarantee it carries, from one aggregate bound over the selected set to a bound per stratum, **which is the only difference** Section 5 (B2$'$) isolates" is false as a description of `exp13`. The arm differs from sIVM on three axes: (i) guarantee target, aggregate against per-stratum; (ii) sampling allocation, 135 uniform draws against 90 FALSE + 45 TRUE via per-stratum shuffles; (iii) stopping rule, one-shot at a budget handed to it after the fact against a doubling look schedule with futility stopping. The competitor is denied adaptivity by construction, since its budget is *defined* as sIVM's realized oracle count. The qualitative conclusion survives, because sIVM wins on error placement for reasons that follow from the guarantee alone, but the isolation claim is the first thing a methods referee tests.

The related and larger point, which B raises and the panel endorses: this repository's own novelty audit designates prompt-delta x row-interaction stratification as the mechanism "no prior system computes," and the paper's own measurements retire it (AUC 0.408 on formatting, and the interaction refinement certifies **less** than value-only, 63.7% against 82.8%). The paper is right to report that and right to pin the value-only stratifier, but it leaves the novelty case resting on guarantee granularity, which is standard subgroup control. **What is actually novel is the estimand: cross-version reuse where the oracle is a fresh draw of the *same* stochastic cell, which creates an intrinsic per-stratum floor with no counterpart in one-shot proxy selection.** Section 2 now measures exactly that (2.9% FALSE, 26.0% TRUE) and Section 3 never says it.

*Required fix:* say what is held fixed (oracle budget, ground-truth label pool, error target, family-wise $\delta$) and what is not (sample allocation, stopping rule, per-test level). ## Part 4: Panel disagreements and meta-reviewer adjudications

Seven points where reviewers diverged or where a claim needed independent verification. All recomputations below were performed by the meta-reviewer against the tree as it stands, including six live MySQL queries.

1. **Which direction does the floor estimator err, and which alternative is correct?** A recomputes under single-draw conditioning and reports the paper **understates** the TRUE floor (26.63% against the published 25.98%). B, C, D and E recompute on the `d2` draws or leave-one-out and report the paper **overstates** it (20.2% to 25.28%). B goes further and calls the published pair "the most flattering of five defensible choices." **Adjudication: every recomputation is arithmetically correct, the disagreement is about the estimand, and B's key-shopping framing is wrong.** The meta-reviewer reproduced six variants from `exp10-labels.json`:

   | Key | Measured on | TRUE | FALSE |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | majority(d1) | d1 pairs (**published**) | 146/562 = 25.98% | 156/5,434 = 2.871% |
   | each TRUE draw as anchor | remaining d1 draws (**certifier's estimand**) | 302/1,134 = 26.63% | 302/10,858 = 2.781% |
   | d1[0] | remaining d1 pair (leave-one-out) | 45/178 = 25.28% | 59/1,820 = 3.242% |
   | majority(d1) | d1 + d2 pairs | 264/1,126 = 23.45% | 334/10,868 = 3.073% |

## What still blocks submission

Stated explicitly, because the vote did not move and the reason matters. **The paper is not submittable today, and six items block it.** None is a redesign, one requires roughly \$0.20 of API spend, and one requires a single live run.
