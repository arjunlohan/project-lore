# sIVM Draft, Round-1 Merged Review: Ranked Revision List

Five independent reviews (theory, systems/baselines, economics, statistics, presentation), merged and deduplicated into one ranked list. Tags: **[MANDATORY]** items must be closed before any submission; **[MAJOR]** and **[MINOR]** are nice-to-have this cycle, with MAJOR items likely to be demanded in review anyway. Reviewer coverage is noted as (N/5). Counts: 12 MANDATORY, 8 MAJOR, 2 MINOR.

## Verdict

Five reviews, one signal. The problem is real and unoccupied (certified reuse across a definition change for stochastic prompt-defined views), the estimand is well-chosen, the empirical posture is unusually honest (refusals reported, realized-vs-nominal shown), and the numbers are largely traceable: multiple reviewers independently reproduced the implementation's empirical-Bernstein bounds to five decimals from the experiment JSONs. Nobody disputes that this paper should exist. Every reviewer nonetheless lands between "reject and resubmit" and "major revision," for converging reasons. The formal core does not survive scrutiny: Theorem 1 bounds the whole-stratum mean while the stated guarantee covers the adversely selected unsampled remainder, and Assumption A3 asserts, rather than proves, without-replacement validity of the i.i.d. empirical-Bernstein bound in runs that sample strata to census. The flagship table is stitched from four certification procedures selected per-experiment after seeing outcomes, contains two cells that trace to no artifact, and contradicts the abstract at alpha = 0.1. The empirics are one label draw per edit pair read at prefix depths and presented as thirty-odd sweeps, of which only 9 are non-vacuous. The novelty claim is undefended against the obvious SUPG/BARGAIN reduction. The economics protect a $0.21 recompute while every regime that would justify certification (frontier pricing, agentic columns, rate limits, corpus scale) goes unmeasured. And Section 5, the paper's center of gravity, is a TODO. All of this is fixable in one focused cycle, several of the highest-leverage fixes cost zero API dollars because the label vectors are frozen, and the fixed paper is plausibly a strong PVLDB submission. Do not submit until every MANDATORY item below is closed.

Note on sequencing: items 1 to 6 change what the experiments and theorem say; item 8 (writing Section 5) should be executed against the rerun artifacts, not the current ones.

## MANDATORY before any submission

### 1. [MANDATORY · reject-level · 5/5] Theorem 1 does not establish its stated guarantee: it bounds the stratum mean, not the reuse set

**Where:** Section 3, Theorem 1 (proof sketch, final step); Section 2, estimand definition; Table 1 realized-FR column.

**Objection:** The theorem's conclusion does not follow from its proof. The EB certificate bounds the whole-stratum mean p_bar_j, but FR(R) averages p_i over only the unsampled rows, since R excludes the sampled set S ("sampled rows are already fresh"). The closing step, "any weighted average of them is <= alpha," silently substitutes stratum means for reuse-subset means. The substitution is not merely loose, it is adversely selected: certification triggers on low observed flips in S, and the complement mean equals (n_j p_bar_j - m_j p_bar_S)/(n_j - m_j), so the event "certified" is positively correlated with an enriched complement, by up to a factor n_j/(n_j - m_j) (4.85x in exp3b, where the certified stratum was 79% sampled, 1440/1814). The paper's own data exhibits the effect: exp3b ground-truth stratum rate 2.81% (51/1814) versus realized reuse-set rate 4.28% (16/374) against alpha = 5%, a 1.5x enrichment consuming 86% of the alpha budget. Constructive counterexample: a 400-row stratum with 40 rows at p_i = 1 and 360 at p_i = 0 has stratum mean exactly alpha = 0.1, yet the unsampled remainder's mean exceeds alpha with probability near 1/2 under the uniform shuffle. No term in the union bound accounts for this failure mode. This is the same selection-bias gap the authors' own verification memo (B3/B4) ordered closed, and it is still open.

**Required fix:** Pick one, state the choice in Section 2, and rerun the table under it (the alpha = 0.05 row is the one at risk):
(a) bound the complement mean directly with a finite-population prediction bound (hypergeometric / without-replacement confidence sequence on the unsampled rows, SUPG-style);
(b) prove the corollary FR(R) <= alpha * n_j/(n_j - m_j) and certify against the deflated level alpha * (n_j - m_j)/n_j;
(c) redefine the estimand over all cells of certified strata, with sampled cells scored at post-recompute error, and state explicitly that the realized-FR column measures that quantity.
Cite Maurer-Pontil at the bound's first use and Bardenet-Maillard where without-replacement behavior is invoked (both sit in the bibliography and are never cited in text). Discuss the exp3b 4.28%-against-5% near-miss honestly: it is this selection effect surfacing in the paper's own data, and it deserves that framing rather than a victory lap.

### 2. [MANDATORY · reject-level · 5/5] Pin ONE certification procedure, regenerate Table 1 mechanically, and reconcile the abstract

**Where:** Section 3 step 3 (look schedule) and the Power paragraph; Assumption A2; Table 1 and caption; abstract finding (ii).

**Objection:** The evaluated system is not one procedure but a family selected per-experiment after seeing outcomes. Reviewers who reproduced the EB bounds recovered four distinct delta-split policies behind Table 1: exp2 single-look delta' = 0.05 (the alpha = 0.2 row), exp3 four-look delta' = 0.0125 (alpha = 0.1), exp3b six-look delta' = 0.008333 (alpha = 0.05), and five-look splits in exp5/exp6. The smooth 88.4 -> 72.7 -> 18.7 -> refusal frontier exists only because the procedure swaps at each alpha: under exp2's procedure held fixed the frontier is 88.45 -> 86.3 -> 0, a cliff; under exp3's it is 0 -> 0 -> 72.7. The alpha = 0.20 headline row is not reproducible under Section 3's stated schedule (the look-1 zero-flip bound at 45 samples with delta' = 0.0125 is 0.269 > 0.20, so certification lands at look 2 with roughly 135 calls and about 86% savings, not 90 calls and 88.4%). The exp3b deep schedule violates the stated sizing rule ("sized so the final look can certify a zero-flip stratum") and was run after the rule-conforming exp3 refused; the composite try-shallow-then-deepen procedure does not enjoy the stated delta (nested tests on shared data need a budget split the paper never states). The stratifier has the same defect: Table 1 pairs lexical-refused with embedding-certified on the same edits and draws, and a lexical-then-embedding fallback violates A2's "fixed before any oracle call," requiring Bonferroni across attempts. Two cells trace to no artifact at all: the formatting alpha = 0.02 row reports 90 oracle calls (exp3 records 639, exp3b records 1,485; no file shows 90), and the widening lexical "any alpha / 90" row contradicts exp3, which spent 405 calls at alpha = 0.1 before refusing. The abstract's "86-88% call savings" quotes exp2's dropped configuration (86.3%, 176 calls) while the table prints 72.7% at the same alpha. The in-text "n* ~ 360 at alpha = 0.05 with our budgets" reproduces at no budget actually used (recomputations give roughly 174 to 313 depending on delta'; 360 is exp3's final look size, not the zero-flip minimum). When a reviewer recomputes the table and two cells fail, trust in the remaining thirty is gone.

**Required fix:** Fix one schedule-generation rule (state |L_j| as a function of n_j, alpha, delta), one delta-allocation rule, one futility rule, and one stratifier policy, all ex ante. If the intended system is a fallback ladder (lexical then embedding, shallow then deep), present the ladder as the procedure and split delta across its stages inside Theorem 1. Rerun every sweep under that single procedure; regenerate every table cell and in-text number mechanically from the JSONs; add a per-row provenance column (source artifact, schedule depth, delta'). Report oracle calls on every refusal path. Recompute the n* example at the stated delta' (and state delta' wherever a sample-size number appears), or present 360 explicitly as the doubling-schedule endpoint that upper-bounds n*. Make the abstract and Table 1 agree. If both shallow and deep schedules are kept, present them as an explicit schedule comparison and decompose the frontier into the alpha effect and the sampling-depth effect (savings = (n_j - m_j)/n falls with depth by construction).

### 3. [MANDATORY · reject-level · 3/5] Repair the formal scaffolding: without-replacement validity of the EB bound, cross-row independence, and the probability space

**Where:** Assumptions A1-A3 (Section 2) and their use in Theorem 1; the estimand paragraph.

**Objection:** Three gaps. (a) A3 embeds an unproven mathematical proposition inside an assumption box: "i.i.d. empirical-Bernstein bounds are conservative in the without-replacement regimes used here." Hoeffding's 1963 convex-ordering reduction transfers Chernoff bounds on the sum from WoR to i.i.d.; it does not automatically transfer Maurer-Pontil, whose empirical-standard-deviation concentration step uses the entropy method and is genuinely i.i.d. The regime matters: the paper's own runs sample strata to census (exp5: 649/649, 639/639, 568/568; exp6: 559/559, 529/529, 467/467), where WoR effects are maximal, and the certified exp5 widening result at alpha = 0.1 rests on a census stratum. Citing Bardenet-Maillard is itself evidence that i.i.d. EB does not apply verbatim under WoR. Every certificate, the n* formula, and the frontier formula run through this inequality; the validity of an inequality is a lemma, not an assumption. (b) A1 states the wrong independence: it constrains repeated draws of one cell, while the EB machinery needs independence across the sampled rows' fresh draws within the certification window. The paper's own nondeterminism mechanism (provider-side batching, kernel-path variation) correlates contemporaneous calls, and the bounded-concurrency runner guarantees sampled cells are computed in overlapping batches, so a batch-level shock violates the concentration inequality exactly when the certificate is computed. (c) The probability space is never pinned (the guarantee must be declared conditional on the realized cache and edit, under which the frozen stratifier, K, and the schedule are measurable); K in delta_j = delta/K is never defined; FR(empty set) is 0/0 and half the table's rows have R empty; and A2's "deterministic" is both stronger than needed and false for an embedding API by the paper's own arguments.

**Required fix:** Prove the binary-case transfer lemma (Hoeffding-63 convex ordering for the mean term plus a monotone-event argument for the empirical-variance term, which is feasible because sigma_hat^2 = p_hat(1 - p_hat) makes the certification event monotone in the sum), or replace Maurer-Pontil with Bardenet-Maillard or Waudby-Smith-Ramdas without-replacement bounds and regenerate all constants (n*, frontier, savings). Restate A3 as a statement about the sampling design only. Add explicit cross-row independence of oracle draws over the certification window to A1, discuss the batching threat and any mitigation (temporally spreading sampled calls), and note the residual risk in Limitations. Add one paragraph fixing the conditioning (conditional-on-cache validity implies the marginal), defining K, stating the FR(empty set) convention (the guarantee is vacuous there), and weakening A2 to "measurable with respect to pre-certification information and independent of certification-window draws."

### 4. [MANDATORY · reject-level · 5/5] Rebuild the empirics on replication: reshuffle bootstraps, fresh label draws, a known-p calibration study, and honest hold accounting

**Where:** Section 5 throughout; Table 1; abstract and caption ("realized false-reuse never exceeded its certified bound in any sweep we ran").

**Objection:** Every number is a single realization. Ground-truth labels were computed once per edit pair, and every sweep is a deterministic prefix-read of one shuffle (exp2's false-value stratum shows 0 flips at n = 45, 0 at 88, 1 at 174; exp3b shows 6 at 360 and 35 at 1440: nested prefixes of one label vector). The paper has five random draws, not 32 sweeps; exp3, exp3b, exp5, and exp7 record no seed at all. The validity headline is close to vacuous: of 37 sweeps carrying an alpha and a reuse count, 27 reused zero cells, so FR = 0/0 trivially satisfies any alpha; 9 non-vacuous certifications remain, and zero violations in 9 heavily correlated trials is consistent, by the rule of three, with a true violation rate as high as 28% against a nominal delta of 10%. The realized-to-nominal ratios (0.14 to 0.86) show the bounds are mostly slack by 3x or more, which is exactly the regime where zero violations carries no information: a loose bound never binds, so it can never be caught being wrong. The experiments as run cannot distinguish "our certificates are valid" from "our certificates are so conservative they are untestable." And the paper's most impressive row is a knife-edge: 35 flips in 1,440 with bound 0.046633 against alpha = 0.05 (margin 0.0034); 39 flips would flip the verdict, and with binomial SD 5.84 on the flip count, P(>= 39) is about 27%, so roughly one reseed in four deletes the row. Its realized FR of 4.28% (16/374) has Wilson 95% CI [2.65%, 6.84%], which straddles alpha: on the row that matters most, the data are consistent with a violation.

**Required fix:** (a) Because the label vectors are frozen, shuffle variability is free: rerun each (edit, alpha, schedule) cell over at least 1,000 reseeded shuffles and report P(certify), median savings, median realized FR, and 5th/95th percentiles. Zero API calls required, so there is no excuse for omitting it. (b) Redraw the oracle labels at least three times per edit pair (about 30K calls, a few dollars at the recorded per-call price) so label-draw variance is separated from shuffle variance. (c) Add a calibration study: semisynthetic strata relabeled from the empirical vectors so the true rate p is known and set to {alpha/2, 0.9 alpha, alpha, 1.1 alpha, 2 alpha}; at least 1,000 trials per configuration; report measured certification-error rate against delta plus the power curve, and report the conservativeness factor (nominal alpha over realized FR) as a first-class result, since it is the actual cost of the Bonferroni-over-strata-and-looks design. (d) Never count an empty reuse set as a "hold"; state the number of non-vacuous certification events; rephrase the never-exceeded claim as a validity sanity check over correlated sweeps sharing one label draw. (e) Put a confidence interval on every realized-FR entry and state that realized FR is a one-draw estimate of FR(R). (f) Report seeds and corpus row IDs for every experiment.

### 5. [MANDATORY · reject-level · 4/5] Implement the SUPG/BARGAIN reduction baseline, and repair B2/B3

**Where:** Section 5, baselines B1-B3; the SUPG/BARGAIN paragraph in related work.

**Objection:** The one baseline that defends the novelty claim is missing. The reduction any PC member of this venue will construct in their head: SUPG/BARGAIN with the stale cache as the proxy and the new prompt as the oracle. False-reuse of a selected set is one minus its precision, and BARGAIN's adaptive sampling certifies precision targets on exactly this shape. B2 (aggregate-only certification) is not that baseline: it keeps the authors' machinery and removes stratification, a deliberately weakened stand-in. The authors' own verification memo (attack A1) says verbatim that if this baseline is missing, the reviewer builds it and rejects. Until sIVM is shown to beat, or provably subsume, the reduction, "first certified cross-version reuse" reads as "first to rename precision certification as maintenance." Two adjacent baseline defects: B2 is reported only at the alphas where it looks bad (exp7 shows aggregate-only achieving 91% savings at alpha = 0.2 with realized aggregate error 6.15%, beating stratified sIVM's 88.4% at the same alpha while holding its bound), and B3 looks misimplemented: with tau = 0.95 and a per-row similarity range of [0.9397, 0.9667] on the widening edit, a GPTCache-style threshold must refuse a substantial fraction of rows, yet the JSON records reusesEverything = true. There is also no tau sweep, so B3 is one arbitrary point rather than a cost/error curve.

**Required fix:** Implement BARGAIN/SUPG-with-stale-cache-as-proxy as a first-class baseline: proxy score = interaction score (plus a no-signal variant), oracle = fresh draw under pi-prime, target = precision of the reuse set. Report savings at matched realized error on all five edit pairs, in Table 1's cost and realized-FR terms. Then state the delta precisely: the reduction has no row-level risk signal derived from the definition delta, and constructing that signal is mechanism 1. Report B2 and B3 at every alpha, including where B2 beats sIVM, and say why the per-stratum estimand is worth the loss. Fix or explain the tau = 0.95 anomaly and report a full tau ROC over [0.90, 0.99].

### 6. [MANDATORY · reject-level · 4/5] Demonstrate a regime where the avoided recompute is worth avoiding, measure the deployment-scale claim, and stop flattering the cost accounting

**Where:** Abstract; Section 1; implementation section; benchmark cost sentence; Limitations final sentence; Table 1 cost columns.

**Objection:** The paper never demonstrates a regime in which the recompute it avoids is worth avoiding. Every measurement uses one budget model at a measured ~$0.000097/call (exp0: $0.07728 for 800 calls): the 2,000-row recompute the certificates protect against costs ~$0.21, the celebrated 88.4%-savings row saves ~$0.17, the intro's 70,000-call horror story prices out at ~$7 (roughly $3.50 on batch tiers), and the benchmark section hands the reviewer the weapon: "Total corpus-side API cost was under $3" for the entire empirical program. The motivations that survive cheap tokens (frontier pricing, agentic columns, provider rate limits, time-to-consistency) appear nowhere as measurements: the word "agentic" never appears, no frontier model is run, and the sole wall-clock claim ("sampling 5-15% of cells beats recomputing 100% by an order of magnitude") has no experiment behind it and is contradicted by the paper's own operating points (certified rows sampled 4.5% to 81%; tight-alpha runs sampled 74-97%). The savings that are demonstrated live at alpha >= 0.1 with delta = 0.1, where the certified bound (at most 10-20% wrong, 90% confidence) is numerically weaker than blind reuse's realized error on the same edits (6.0% formatting, 9.45% widening); at alpha = 0.05 the method spends 1,626 of 2,000 calls to save 374. Refusal, the expensive path, is priced nowhere: exp5's synonym run at alpha = 0.1 sampled 1,939 of 2,000 cells and certified nothing, 97% of recompute-all cost spent to learn "no," and sequential looks mean that under a rate cap the certifier strictly delays the recompute that refusal makes inevitable; 5 of Table 1's 13 rows are refusals. The cost accounting also flatters the method: the "oracle calls" column excludes recomputation (the alpha = 0.2 row issues 231 calls, not 90). Meanwhile the rescue is cheap and the authors own the assets: n* is constant per stratum, so on the 89,184- and 210,250-row corpora the same ~1,500 calls are ~2% of the column and tight-alpha savings approach ~98%; Section 3 calls large tables "precisely the deployment regime" and no experiment goes there, though ground-truthing one full-scale edit costs under $25 at the paper's own prices.

**Required fix:** (a) At least one measured expensive regime, promoted to the abstract: a frontier-priced model (at ~$0.005/cell a 70K-row rebuild is ~$350) with per-edit dollar accounting at alpha <= 0.05 against a batch-repriced recompute-all baseline; or an agentic/tool-augmented column where per-cell cost is dominated by tool calls; or wall-clock time-to-certified-consistency under an enforced real-world rate limit on >= 70K rows, measured for one certifying and one refusing edit against recompute-now. (b) The scaling run: certification on at least one full-corpus column (89K and/or 210K rows) at alpha in {0.02, 0.05}, reporting savings, dollars, wall-clock, and realized FR spot-checked on a labeled holdout; make that the headline table, and add the savings-versus-table-size curve (n in {2K, 10K, 50K, 89K}), which turns the deployment argument from a formula into a measurement. (c) Split the cost column into certification calls / recompute calls / total calls, add dollars per edit, report calls on every refusal path, and delete or re-derive the 5-15% sentence from the actual table. (d) If the full-scale run cannot be produced this cycle, re-headline the paper around certified change-detection (90-405 calls to learn that a benign-looking edit flipped 9% of a column), which is the genuinely useful cheap product the data already support.

### 7. [MANDATORY · major · 5/5] Restate the impossibility floor per stratum and conditional on the cache; relabel the alpha = 0.02 refusal; run the identity-edit control

**Where:** Section 2, "The impossibility floor"; abstract findings (i) and (iii); Table 1 outcome column "refused (below floor)"; exp0/exp0b.

**Objection:** The floor is stated at the wrong granularity, in the wrong probability space, and is contradicted by the paper's own data. "No procedure whose oracle is a single fresh draw can certify any stratum at alpha < s" uses the column-level self-flip rate s = 3.0%, but instability concentrates: all 6 of exp0's self-flips are positive-involved (positive-pair flip rate 33%; cached-FALSE 0/182, upper bound near 2%), the v=false stratum flips at 2.4-2.8% under a formatting edit, below the claimed floor, and Djinni's 13 self-flips are all adjacent-tier. A stratum of decode-stable rows certifies below s, so the sentence is false as written; the abstract states the correct granularity and Section 2 the wrong one. The argument also marginalizes over the cache draw while the estimand conditions on the realized cache (conditional on c_i, the identity-edit flip probability is 1 - f_V(c_i | r_i), which equals the self-flip rate only in expectation over the cache draw), and the floor is a property of the single-fresh-draw estimand, a definition, not an impossibility theorem about procedures. Consequently Table 1's alpha = 0.02 outcome "refused (below floor)" mislabels a futility stop: the stratum's measured 2.43% exceeds alpha = 0.02; no 3.0% decode floor is involved. The floors themselves are n = 200 point estimates carrying a stated "law": 6 and 13 events, 95% CIs roughly [1.1-1.4%, 6.4%] and [3.8%, 10.8%], so the "formatting flips at roughly 2x the intrinsic floor on both corpora" ratio is compatible with anything from ~0.9x to ~4.3x. The floor is also measured on the wrong object (exp0 uses prompt v2, seed 43, 200 rows, one replicate pair, while the anchored edits act on different 2,000-row samples; the correct null is the paired identity edit pi -> pi on the same rows, never run), and the abstract leads with the 9.5% default-temperature figure from a decode configuration no experiment uses.

**Required fix:** State the floor per stratum and per realized cache: no delta-valid certifier certifies alpha below the stratum's conditional mean disagreement rate, except with probability delta. Derive the column-level identity-edit version (= s) as a corollary in expectation over the cache draw; reconcile Section 2 with the abstract; present vote-of-k explicitly as a change of estimand, not an escape from the floor. Report measured s_j by cached value with CIs (exp0/exp0b contain the breakdown). Run the identity-edit control pi -> pi end to end through the full pipeline at every alpha on the same 2,000 rows, per prompt version, and report savings and realized FR for it, with at least 5 replicate draws per row rather than 1. Relabel the alpha = 0.02 outcome as a futility stop. Reframe finding (i) as "instability concentrates at the class boundary." Demote the 2x claim to an observation on two columns pending a second model family. Move the default-temperature number out of the lead. Add a prevalence-sensitivity experiment (columns at roughly 10%, 30%, 50% positive rate), since certifiable savings depend on positive-class prevalence.

### 9. [MANDATORY · reject-level · 2/5] Unconfound the stratifier story: matched budgets, matched schedules, degeneracy disclosure, and the anti-signal AUC

**Where:** Section 3 step 2 (Stratify); Contribution 2; abstract finding (iv); Table 1 embedding rows; Section 5 baseline B1.

**Objection:** The claim that "stratifier quality is a pure savings lever" is confounded with oracle budget and evaluated only where it can win. The lexical-vs-embedding widening comparison at alpha = 0.2 pits 90 oracle calls against 978 (a 10.9x budget difference attributed entirely to the stratifier). Recomputing with the paper's own bound and observed flip counts: the lexical stratum v=false|o=3+ (size 1,801, p_hat = 25/360 = 0.0694) has Maurer-Pontil upper bound 0.142-0.149 under the paper's own delta splits, well below alpha = 0.2, so the lexical stratifier certifies that stratum with 405 total calls and reuses 1,441 cells, about 72% savings, against the embedding stratifier's 46.1% at 978 calls. On the paper's own data, under a matched schedule, the direction of finding (iv) plausibly reverses. The embedding stratifier is run on exactly the two edit pairs where lexical refused, never on formatting-only (where the interaction AUC is 0.407, an anti-signal) or on either Djinni edit. The lexical interaction partition is degenerate in four of five edit pairs (exp2 formatting: every row o=0; exp2 synonym and exp1 widening: every row o=3+; exp6 Djinni formatting: every row o=0), reducing to cached-value-only; the entire measured evidence for the flagship interaction mechanism is one stratum in one edit (Djinni Junior|o=1-2 at 2.8% versus o=3+ at 9.6%, z = 3.14, p = 0.0017). Uniform delta-splitting also penalizes finer stratifiers by construction (K strata x looks divides delta), so the "pure savings lever" claim is not true even in principle. B1's evidence is misread: AUC 0.407 with 120 positives and 1,880 negatives has Hanley-McNeil SE 0.0251, 95% CI [0.358, 0.457], z = -3.68 against chance, a statistically significant anti-signal (inverting the score yields 0.593), not "no signal"; no AUC has a CI; and B1 covers 2 of 5 edits. The embedding variant's tertile cut points are fit on the 2,000 evaluation rows rather than the corpus the deployment story assumes.

**Required fix:** Run the full matrix: {value-only, lexical, embedding, random, adversarial anti-correlated} stratifiers x all five edit pairs x the alpha grid, under an identical schedule, identical delta-allocation, and matched oracle budget. Report savings at equal cost, including the cells where the embedding stratifier loses. Report, for every edit, how many non-empty interaction buckets the stratifier produced, and state plainly that the lexical variant is degenerate in four of five. Report a within-value-class risk-separation metric (flip-rate spread across interaction buckets, with CIs) as the mechanism's primary evidence, since end-to-end savings conflate mechanism and schedule. Report AUC with CIs for all five edits, both scores, and both stratifier variants; rewrite the B1 sentence to say the interaction score is anti-predictive on formatting, and either explain or exploit the inversion (a sign-corrected or learned score is a real improvement worth measuring). Compute tertile cut points on the full corpus. Put the embedding cost in the ledger. The adversarial anti-correlated stratifier is also the verification memo's own B4 mandate: run it and show realized FR still holds.

## MAJOR (nice-to-have this cycle; expect reviewers to demand them)

### 13. [MAJOR · 5/5] Generality: a second model family behind the "laws," and a real test behind "statistically indistinguishable"

Every empirical law rests on n = 1 model family and two columns. The 2x-floor law is two ratios from one provider's serving stack; a near-deterministic provider collapses the floor and reopens tight alpha, a noisier one erases the formatting savings; corpus replication is not model replication. "Statistically indistinguishable" (12.30% versus 12.45%) is asserted with no test (unpaired SE of the difference ~1.04pp: uninformative in both directions), and "the governing variable is column-type stability, not edit class" is identified from two columns that differ simultaneously in output arity, input length, corpus, prompt, and positive-class prevalence. Fix: replicate exp0, exp2, and exp6 (the floors and the flip-rate matrix) on at least two additional families, one frontier and one open-weight; until then, keep the case-study voice required by items 7 and 12 (no "law," no "governs everything"). Run McNemar on discordant pairs plus TOST with a pre-stated equivalence margin (e.g., 3pp). To keep finding (v) as stated, add at least four more columns crossing arity (binary, 4-way, 10-way) x input length x subjectivity and show flip rate tracks self-flip rate with a fitted slope and CI; otherwise demote it to an observation about two columns.

### 14. [MAJOR · 2/5] Repair the vCache dismissal: the AUC argument cannot carry its "therefore"

AUC 0.467/0.499 on raw cross-version similarity kills any monotone threshold on that feature (AUC is invariant to monotone transforms), but vCache's mechanism is a per-entry learned threshold, not a monotone transform of a global score, so "adding a version key therefore cannot produce a sound cross-version policy" is a non sequitur; as written it is attackable as a strawman. The structurally correct kill sits in the authors' own memo (A3): one delta applied to N rows gives each cache entry exactly one query ever, so there is no per-entry query stream to learn thresholds from; pooling across entries turns the learner into a global function of row and delta features, which converges to the paper's own interaction model; and vCache's per-lookup guarantee does not compose into a set-level false-reuse bound over a finite population. Fix: replace the "therefore" with the structural argument, implement vCache-with-version-key with pooled threshold learning as an actual baseline (memo-mandated), and report every AUC with a confidence interval (roughly 120-189 positive labels per edit).

### 15. [MAJOR · 2/5] Strengthen the futility rule and price refusal

The futility rule (stop when the point estimate exceeds alpha) failed both ways. It did not stop near-frontier runs: exp5 synonym at alpha = 0.1 consumed 1,939 of 2,000 calls and refused; exp6 formatting at alpha = 0.1 burned 855 calls for zero savings; a certifier whose failure mode costs ~100% of the thing it is supposed to avoid is a first-order operational property, not a footnote. And it leaks power: at n = 45 with true rate 0.04 and alpha = 0.05, P(p_hat > alpha) is about 0.27, so roughly a quarter of certifiable strata are abandoned by noise, and this is presented as a feature. Fix: base futility on a lower confidence bound rather than the point estimate; add budget-capped looks or cross-strata early abort; measure the false-futility rate from the existing label vectors (free); add a cost-of-refusal analysis (feeding item 6c) and state plainly that near-frontier attempts can cost full recompute.

### 16. [MAJOR · 2/5] Statistical hygiene: error bars, a delta sweep, and an FCR correction

No error bars anywhere: every flip rate, savings figure, realized FR, and AUC is a bare point estimate (formatting 6.0% is [5.0%, 7.1%]; Djinni realized FR 6.53% is [4.5%, 9.5%]). Delta is fixed at 0.1 in every experiment, so the paper never demonstrates delta does anything, and 0.1 is loose for a guarantee pitched at production. Per-stratum empirical rates are displayed only for strata that survived certification and futility, textbook winner's curse, and the FCR correction the authors' own memo lists as mandatory (B4) is absent. Fix: CIs on every rate in every table and figure; sweep delta in {0.2, 0.1, 0.05, 0.01} and show the sampling-cost and savings response; apply a Benjamini-Yekutieli FCR correction to reported per-stratum rates, or flag them explicitly as selection-conditional.

### 17. [MAJOR · 1/5] Report the user-visible slice: not one cached-TRUE cell is ever certified

Across every sweep on the boolean column, the v=true strata are refused in exp1, exp2, exp3, exp3b, and exp5 (single-shot flip 37.1%, still 24.7% under vote-of-3, EB upper bound 0.38). In the motivating recruiter table, the TRUEs are the shortlist, the only cells a user actually reads: "88% call savings" containing 0% of the user-visible cells is a far weaker product claim than the abstract implies, and uniform per-stratum alpha ignores wildly asymmetric cell utility. Fix: report savings separately for the positive/shortlist slice in every results table; either add utility-weighted alpha allocation across strata or state plainly that on selective boolean columns, positives are effectively always recomputed.

### 18. [MAJOR · 2/5] Ground the edit workload: frequencies, realism, and expected savings over a stream of edits

The 86-88% headline rests on a formatting-only edit whose text never appears in the paper and whose class is the one users least make; every behavior-adjacent edit (synonym 9.05%, widening 9.45%, criteria 12.45% flip rates) certifies 0-46% savings and only at alpha >= 0.1-0.2. No edit-workload distribution is given: SPADE's nine empirically derived classes from 19 real pipelines are cited but not used for frequencies, and lore, claimed as a production system, presumably has real prompt-edit logs that are never mined. A reader cannot compute expected savings over a realistic stream of edits, which, given refusals still consume 90-1,939 calls plus sequential latency, could plausibly be near zero at tight alpha; as it stands the headline number is the method grading an exam it wrote for itself. Fix: source class frequencies from SPADE or from lore's own edit logs; map or seed the four author-invented edit classes to SPADE's taxonomy; report expected calls saved per edit under that distribution at each alpha. (Printing the five edit prompts verbatim is covered by item 10.)

### 19. [MAJOR · 1/5] Define certification under edit sequences, and run the chain the data already contain

The paper certifies a single edit v to v-prime and is silent on what every real user does next: edit again. After certification, reused cells are draws from f_V while recomputed cells are draws from f_V-prime, so the next certification runs against a mixture of generations. The per-edit estimand remains well defined, the memo even names the resolution (cache-as-is versus current-definition certification, non-compounding), and the data contain a chain (formatting v1 -> v2, then synonym v2 -> v3 on the same column), unexercised. Fix: add a short subsection defining certification against the as-is cache under the current definition, argue non-compounding of the guarantee across edits, and run the v1 -> v2 -> v3 chain from exp2 as evidence.

### 20. [MAJOR · 2/5] Scope the slice guarantee honestly, and run the B2 attack against sIVM itself

"Per-stratum bounds are what make the guarantee mean something for every user-visible slice of the table" overclaims: the bound is inherited only by unions of whole certified strata; any cross-cutting filter (country, seniority, tenure decile) can concentrate a certified stratum's alpha budget and carry error above alpha with no violation of Theorem 1. The paper's own B2 attack (34.9% error concealed in a reused subgroup) applies one level down to sIVM; the argument as written proves too much. Fix: scope the claim to stratum-aligned slices and say explicitly that cross-cutting slices are not covered. Then run the honest version of the attack against sIVM's own certified strata: take the alpha = 0.2 formatting run and report realized FR broken down by two or three unmodeled covariates (labels exist, zero API cost); reporting a slice that exceeds alpha, with an honest framing of what per-stratum guarantees do and do not promise, is far stronger than leaving the reviewer to construct it.

## MINOR (polish)

### 21. [MINOR · 2/5] Power-paragraph caveats: the full near-frontier bound and the mechanical savings cap

The frontier formula n >~ 2p(1-p) ln(2/delta')/(alpha - p)^2 is correct as an order statement but should be labeled the constant-power threshold; it omits the additive 7 ln(2/delta')/(3(n-1)) term that dominates as p -> 0 (the truncated form wrongly predicts n -> 0); and the savings collapse near tight alpha is partly mechanical, since savings are capped at (n_j - m_j)/n before any statistics (at m = 1440 of n = 2000 the cap is 28%, so 18.7% is near the ceiling: the frontier partly measures "we sampled more," not "alpha got tighter"). Fix: write the full bound, name the constant-power caveat, and state the mechanical cap alongside the frontier narrative.
